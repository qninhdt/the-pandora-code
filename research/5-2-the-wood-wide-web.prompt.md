# Deep Research - The wood-wide web (the-wood-wide-web)

## Goal

This is the **second chapter of Part III** (after *What Eywa is*), and it teaches
**two payloads at once**:

- **Pandora payload:** the *physical mechanism* of how Pandora's plant life is
  literally wired together underground - the **root-coupling** between the
  **Tree of Souls (Vitraya Ramunong)**, the **Tree of Voices (Utral
  Aymokriyä)**, **Hometree (Kelutral)**, and the wider forest. Where *What Eywa
  is* asked "is the network a mind?", this chapter asks the more concrete
  question: **what is the wiring made of, and how does a signal actually travel
  through it?** Canon gives us Grace Augustine's line about "electrochemical
  communication between the roots of the trees" and a forest where the
  connections "outnumber the synapses in a human brain"; this chapter takes that
  literally and asks how a planet-spanning root/signal network could be built,
  what carries the signal, how trees that are kilometres apart could be coupled,
  and whether the sacred trees are special *hubs* or just ordinary nodes. Treat
  the underground network as an **engineering and biology problem** to be
  characterized from canon, not a mysticism to be admired.
- **STEM payload:** the real science of **mycorrhizal networks** - the symbiosis
  between plant roots and fungi, and the "**wood-wide web**" hypothesis that
  these fungal threads connect trees into a shared belowground network. Cover the
  biology of **mycorrhizae** (arbuscular vs ectomycorrhizal symbiosis; the
  fungal **mycelium** and **hyphae**; how the plant-fungus trade works -
  carbon/sugars from the tree for nitrogen, phosphorus, and water from the
  fungus), the landmark and contested research (**Suzanne Simard's** isotope-
  tracer experiments on carbon transfer between Douglas firs and birch and the
  "**mother tree**" idea; **Toby Kiers'** work on **biological markets**,
  reciprocal trade, and even "**punishment**"/sanctions between partners; the
  network-mapping work that gave us the "wood-wide web" map), the **signalling**
  question (do trees really send defence/warning signals and resources to kin
  through fungi, and how strong is the evidence?), and - crucially - the **2023
  scientific backlash** (Karst, Jones & Hoeksema's review arguing the popular
  claims about common mycorrhizal networks are overstated and under-evidenced).
  The reader should leave understanding what mycorrhizal networks *demonstrably
  do*, what is *hypothesized but unproven*, and how to tell solid science from a
  beautiful story that ran ahead of the data.

Research must cover BOTH sides thoroughly and keep them cleanly separable.
Breadth is welcome - the author filters aggressively.

## Part B - Earth-science literature (mycorrhizal networks & the wood-wide web)

The real science, explained from credible/primary sources, at a level a curious
non-specialist can follow. The spine is: *what is the wood-wide web, what can it
provably do, and where did the popular story run ahead of the evidence?*

1. **Mycorrhizae - the root-fungus symbiosis (the foundation).** What a
   mycorrhiza *is*: the ancient (~400+ million year) symbiosis between plant
   roots and fungi. Cover the **two main types**: **arbuscular mycorrhizae (AM)**
   (fungi penetrate root cells; the majority of plant species; *Glomeromycota*)
   vs **ectomycorrhizae (EM)** (fungi sheathe the root tips; many temperate/
   boreal trees - the type most associated with "wood-wide web" forests). Explain
   the **mycelium** and **hyphae** (the fungal threads), and the basic **trade**:
   the plant supplies **carbon (sugars from photosynthesis)**; the fungus
   supplies **water, nitrogen, phosphorus** and other minerals its vast hyphal
   surface area can reach that roots cannot. Give the scale: how much of a
   plant's nutrient uptake can route through fungi; how vast a mycelial network
   is per cubic metre of soil. This is the **real, settled** science - the bridge
   to Pandora's "roots that connect everything."

2. **The "wood-wide web" hypothesis - common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs).** The
   step from "each tree has fungal partners" to "**fungi link multiple trees into
   a shared network**." Define a **common mycorrhizal network (CMN)**: a single
   fungal mycelium colonizing the roots of two or more plants simultaneously,
   potentially allowing transfer *between* plants. Trace where the term
   "**wood-wide web**" came from (the 1997 *Nature* cover and news framing of
   Simard's work). Be precise about the **claim ladder**, because the chapter's
   honesty depends on it:
   - (a) fungi physically connect to multiple trees - **well supported**;
   - (b) materials (carbon, nutrients, water) can move *through* fungal links
     between plants - **demonstrated under some conditions**;
   - (c) trees use these networks to *purposefully* share resources with kin,
     send *warning signals*, and *nurture seedlings* - **popular, influential,
     but scientifically contested**.
   Keep these three rungs separate throughout.

3. **Suzanne Simard's research & the "mother tree."** The landmark experiments:
   **dual isotope labelling** (¹³C / ¹⁴C) showing **bidirectional carbon
   transfer between Douglas fir and paper birch** (Simard et al., *Nature* 1997);
   the later body of work on **"mother trees"** (large hub trees preferentially
   connected, claimed to channel carbon to seedlings, recognize kin, and pass on
   resources as they die). Explain the experiments clearly - what was measured,
   what the isotopes showed, the magnitude of transfer (often small, net flows
   debated). Then state the popularization (Simard's *Finding the Mother Tree*;
   the TED talk; the influence on *Avatar*-style "the forest is one connected
   organism" thinking) **and** the scientific caution about how far the data
   actually reach. The "mother tree" is the single closest real-world analog to a
   **Tree of Souls hub** - so get both the appeal and the critique.

4. **Toby Kiers - biological markets, reciprocity, and "punishment."** The
   counter-narrative to pure altruistic sharing: Kiers' work treating the
   symbiosis as a **biological market** where plant and fungus **trade** and each
   can **reward cooperative partners and sanction cheats** (e.g., plants directing
   more carbon to fungi that deliver more phosphorus, and vice versa; reciprocal
   reward; evidence of resource transfer following **trade**/market dynamics, even
   price-like responses to local scarcity). Recent network-imaging work (the
   SPUN / Kiers lab visualizing nutrient flows through living mycelium). This
   reframes the network not as a kumbaya "trees help each other" but as an
   **economy** - a richer, more accurate model the chapter can use against the
   sentimental reading.

5. **The 2023 backlash - what is actually proven (CRITICAL for honesty).** The
   most important corrective in this field: **Karst, Jones & Hoeksema (2023,
   *Nature Ecology & Evolution*), "Positive citation bias and overinterpreted
   results lead to misinformation about common mycorrhizal networks in forests."**
   Summarize their three core findings: (1) the **structure/functioning** of CMNs
   in forests is **far less established** than popular accounts imply; (2)
   claimed **effects of CMNs on seedlings/transfer are inconsistent** and often
   not robust; and (3) there is **citation bias** - papers increasingly cite the
   exciting positive claims while overlooking null/contradictory results, so the
   "story" calcified faster than the evidence. Also cover the broader critique:
   that **field demonstrations** that resources move *preferentially to kin via a
   shared fungal link* (rather than leaking into soil and being taken up
   independently) are hard and rare; that "mother tree" intentionality is an
   **anthropomorphism risk**; and the methodological problem of distinguishing
   *transfer through the network* from *transfer through soil*. The chapter needs
   this to avoid repeating the pop-sci overreach - it should celebrate the real
   network while being straight about the contested claims.

6. **Do trees "talk" and "warn" through fungi? (signalling).** The evidence on
   **defence signalling** through mycorrhizal networks - e.g., experiments
   suggesting aphid-attacked plants prime neighbours' defences via fungal
   connections (Babikova et al. 2013), and analogous claims for stress/warning
   signals. Treat carefully: what was actually measured, sample sizes, whether it
   replicates, and the difference between **a real signalling pathway** and **a
   compelling anecdote**. Contrast with **better-established** plant communication
   channels (airborne volatile organic compounds) so the reader sees fungal
   signalling is *one contested channel among several*, not the only way plants
   coordinate.

7. **How a signal/resource physically moves - the mechanism.** For pairing with
   canon's "electrochemical" claim: explain the **actual transport mechanisms**
   in fungal networks - **cytoplasmic streaming** and mass flow of solutes
   through hyphae, source-sink gradients (carbon flows from high to low
   concentration), and the **timescales** (slow - hours to days, not the
   millisecond electrochemistry of a neuron). Note that fungi and plants *do*
   have **electrical signalling** (action-potential-like depolarizations along
   roots and hyphae; recent work on electrical spiking in fungi) but that it is
   **slow and low-bandwidth** compared to a brain. This is the honest reality
   check on canon's "outnumber the synapses in a human brain" line: **a network
   can be enormous in connection count yet glacially slow and low-capacity** - a
   point the chapter sets up here and `the-bandwidth-of-a-planet` will quantify.

8. **Analogies & contrasts that connect the science back to Pandora.** Good
   bridges for the author:
   - The **mycorrhizal network as the real-world model for Pandora's
     root-coupling** - canon says "roots connect," real forests achieve
     tree-to-tree coupling not by roots touching but by a **third party (fungi)**
     threading between them; this is the elegant fix for "how do distant trees
     connect" that canon never spells out.
   - The **"mother tree" / hub tree** as the model for a **Tree of Souls** - and
     the caution that hub-tree intentionality is exactly the over-read the 2023
     critique flags.
   - The **biological-market** framing as a more sophisticated lens than "the
     forest is one harmonious organism" - the network is real, but it is an
     **economy of trade and sanctions**, not a hive mind.
   - The **slow, low-bandwidth** reality of fungal transport as the honest
     counterweight to canon's brain comparison.
   - The **honest verdict** the chapter should be able to reach: mycorrhizal
     networks are **real, ancient, and ecologically important**, and they make
     Pandora's "the roots are all connected" far *less* fanciful than it sounds -
     but the leap from "connected" to "a single coordinating super-organism" is
     exactly the leap real science has *not* yet earned, on Earth or on Pandora.
