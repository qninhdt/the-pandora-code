# Deep Research - From Stardust to Pandora (from-stardust-to-pandora)

## Goal

This chapter teaches **two payloads at once**:

- **Pandora payload:** what official Avatar material actually establishes about
  **where Pandora, Polyphemus and the Alpha Centauri system came from** — the
  bodies' masses, radii, densities, bulk compositions, atmospheric inventories,
  mineral endowment (including unobtanium), stated or implied ages, sibling moons,
  and any statement bearing on how the system formed. Canon opens *in medias res*:
  a finished moon with a breathable-looking sky, a superconducting orebody and a
  gas giant filling the horizon. This chapter asks the question the films never
  ask — what had to happen, over roughly thirteen billion years, for those objects
  to exist at all. It must treat Pandora as an object with a formation history that
  can be reasoned about from its measured properties, while stating plainly where
  canon supplies no mass, no age, no composition and no formation account.
- **STEM payload:** the real **cosmic chemical and physical genealogy of a
  habitable world**: Big Bang nucleosynthesis and why the young universe held
  almost nothing but hydrogen and helium; stellar structure and the fusion
  sequences that build carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and the iron peak; the explosive
  and neutron-capture channels (core-collapse supernovae, Type Ia, AGB s-process,
  neutron-star-merger r-process) that make everything heavier; how enriched gas
  returns to the interstellar medium and raises the metallicity of each new
  stellar generation; how a molecular cloud collapses into a protostar plus a
  protoplanetary disk; how dust becomes pebbles, planetesimals, cores and giant
  planets; how a giant planet builds a satellite system in its own circumplanetary
  disk; and how carbonaceous material delivers the volatiles and organics a moon
  needs. The reader should leave able to look at any rock, any lungful of air and
  any living body and trace its atoms to specific astrophysical events — and able
  to say which of those steps Pandora's stated properties make easy and which they
  make very hard.

Research must cover BOTH sides thoroughly and keep them separable. Breadth is
welcome — the author will filter aggressively.

**Scope guard (avoid overlap with neighbouring chapters):** This chapter is about
**origins — nucleosynthesis, cloud collapse, disk formation, accretion and
volatile delivery — ending the moment Pandora exists as a differentiated body with
an atmosphere.** Do **NOT**:

- re-run the Alpha Centauri **orbital architecture**, circumstellar habitable-zone
  boundaries, exomoon detection methods, or the dynamical stability of Pandora's
  orbit — those belong to “Where is Pandora?”. Use the system's stellar masses,
  ages, metallicities and binary separation only as *formation* inputs: how a disk
  survives, gets truncated, or fails to form around each component.
- re-run tidal locking, libration, eclipse geometry, synodic periods or any part
  of Pandora's calendar — those belong to “Time on Pandora.”

- teach **abiogenesis** — prebiotic chemistry, the RNA world, protocells, metabolic
  first-steps — that is “Origins of life on Pandora.” This chapter delivers the
  *inventory*: which elements and molecules were available, in what abundances, and
  how they arrived. It stops at the doorstep of the first self-replicator.
- teach **biochemical compatibility**, chirality, amino-acid inventories or XNA
  backbones — “What is Pandoran life made of?” owns those. Chirality appears here
  only as a property of *abiotically delivered* meteoritic organics.
- teach **superconductivity**, Cooper pairs, the Meissner effect or flux pinning —
  “Floating mountains, unobtanium and the Flux Devil” owns unobtanium's physics.
  Here, unobtanium matters only as a *cosmochemical* problem: what nucleosynthetic
  channel and what disk or planetary process could concentrate an exotic,
  presumably heavy-element-bearing phase in a moon's crust at ore grade.
- reconstruct Pandora's **geological chronology**, strata, crater counts,
  radiometric dating practice or molecular clocks — that is “Pandora's deep time.”
  Radioactive decay appears here only as *nucleosynthetic yield* (which isotopes
  were made, in what ratio, by which channel) and as the initial radiogenic heat
  budget a freshly formed body inherits.
- model **tidal dissipation**, Io analogues or present-day volcanism — that is
  “What keeps Pandora volcanically alive?”. Accretional heat, core formation and
  short-lived-radionuclide heating belong here only as the *initial* thermal state.
- teach **plate tectonics**, mantle convection or seismology — that is “A crust that
  moves.” Differentiation into core, mantle and crust belongs here; its subsequent
  behaviour does not.
- teach **biopolymers, biomechanics or structural materials** — “Materials of life”
  owns those.
- narrate the **RDA, ISV transit, mining economics or colonial history**. Human
  arrival is out of scope entirely; if unobtanium's market value is mentioned at
  all, it is only to motivate why its cosmochemical origin is worth asking about.

## Part A - Pandora canon

What official Avatar material establishes about Pandora's, Polyphemus's and Alpha
Centauri's **physical properties and origins**. Be specific, cite sources, and
distinguish official canon (the films, *James Cameron's Avatar: An Activist
Survival Guide*, *The Art of Avatar*, official Pandorapedia, Lightstorm/Disney
material, Cameron's own statements) from licensed continuity, game material,
fan-wiki claims and pure visual inference. Where a number appears only in a
promotional graphic or a wiki with no upstream source, say so explicitly.

1. **Pandora's bulk physical parameters.** Collect every stated or strongly implied
   value for radius/diameter, mass, surface gravity, mean density, moment of
   inertia, escape velocity, rotation period and axial tilt. Surface gravity is the
   most commonly quoted figure (frequently ~0.8 g) — trace it to its earliest
   authoritative source and record how radius and density were derived from or
   asserted alongside it. Then work the inverse problem explicitly: given the
   quoted gravity and size, what mean density follows, and what does that density
   imply about the rock/metal/ice/water proportions of the interior? Compare against
   Earth (5.51 g/cm³), Mars, Ganymede, Titan, Europa and Io. Flag any internal
   inconsistency between quoted mass, radius and gravity.
2. **Pandora's bulk and surface composition.** What does official material say about
   crustal rock types, silicates, metals, ice, water inventory, ocean volume, ore
   deposits, soil chemistry and the presence of an intrinsic or induced magnetic
   field? Is there any statement about a metallic core, differentiation, or the
   moon's interior structure? Is Pandora described as rocky, icy, or mixed?
3. **Atmospheric inventory as a formation clue.** Record the canonical atmospheric
   composition and total pressure (commonly given as nitrogen-dominated with
   elevated CO₂ plus trace H₂S, xenon and others; the pressure is usually stated as
   roughly 20% above Earth's). Trace each figure to a source. Then treat the
   inventory as evidence about *origin*: which components suggest outgassing from
   the interior, which suggest late delivery by icy or carbonaceous bodies, which
   suggest ongoing biological or volcanic replenishment, and which are hard to
   reconcile with any of those. Note that a *high* xenon abundance is unusual and
   worth flagging as a cosmochemical puzzle rather than a throwaway detail. Do not
   re-teach the physiology of breathing that air.
4. **Unobtanium as a cosmochemical object.** Gather everything official on
   unobtanium: where it occurs, at what concentration and grade, its stated density,
   its crystal habit, its claimed room-temperature superconductivity, its
   association with the Hallelujah Mountains' magnetic environment, and any claim
   about how it formed. Is it described as an element, a compound, an alloy, or a
   structured phase? Is any atomic number, isotope or synthesis route ever implied?
   Distinguish sharply between what an official source asserts and what has been
   back-filled by fans. Then state the cosmochemical problem plainly: what would
   have to be true of the parent stars, the disk, or the moon's differentiation for
   such a phase to exist and be concentrated at economically minable grade — and
   flag where canon simply provides no answer.
5. **Polyphemus.** Collect stated mass, radius, density, composition, ring system,
   satellite count (Pandora is usually one of many moons), atmospheric appearance,
   internal heat, magnetic field strength and any claim about how it formed. It is
   commonly described as a gas giant of roughly Jupiter-class or larger mass; trace
   the actual quoted figures. Where does Polyphemus orbit relative to its star, and
   is there any claim about migration from a colder formation location? A gas giant
   at close orbital distance is a formation puzzle — record whether canon
   acknowledges it.
6. **Pandora's siblings and the wider system.** Inventory the other named moons of
   Polyphemus and any stated properties. A satellite *system* with a regular,
   coplanar, prograde architecture implies a circumplanetary disk; captured or
   irregular moons imply something else. Record what canon supports.
7. **Alpha Centauri as canon uses it.** What do the films and official material say
   about the star(s) Pandora's system orbits — which component, the binary or
   trinary configuration, distance, brightness, colour, and whether both stars are
   ever depicted in the sky? Note where canon conflicts with the real Alpha Centauri
   system, and where it is simply silent. Keep orbital-stability analysis out of
   scope; this is about the *stellar* inputs to formation.
8. **Any canonical statement about age or history.** Search for any claim about how
   old Pandora, Polyphemus, or the system is; about early bombardment, giant
   impacts, or a formation narrative; and about how long life has existed there.
   Include *An Activist Survival Guide*, production notes, Cameron interviews,
   scriptment material, deleted scenes, tie-in games and companion books, labelling
   each by tier. If there is no canonical age, state that as the central gap.
9. **The biosphere as a compositional constraint.** Without straying into
   biochemistry, record what canon establishes about the *elements* Pandoran life
   uses: carbon-based organic chemistry, liquid water as solvent, and any stated
   unusual elemental requirement. This constrains what the moon's building
   materials had to contain.
10. **Where canon is silent or self-contradictory — list and rank the gaps.** Likely
    candidates to verify include: no stated mass or density; gravity quoted without
    a consistent radius; no age for the moon, the planet or the system; no formation
    account of any kind; no isotopic or elemental abundance data; no explanation for
    unobtanium's origin; unexplained atmospheric xenon; unspecified interior
    structure; unclear whether Pandora formed in place around Polyphemus or was
    captured; Polyphemus's own formation distance unstated; and Alpha Centauri's
    real observed metallicity never reconciled with the fiction. Rank each gap by
    how much it matters to a formation story, and give the strongest bounded
    inference each gap permits **without converting the inference into canon**.

## Part B - Earth-science literature (nucleosynthesis, star and planet formation, volatile delivery)

Explain the real science from credible and preferably primary sources at a level a
curious non-specialist can follow. Use named observations, missions, experiments,
quantitative ranges and dates wherever they illuminate a mechanism. Throughout,
distinguish a **general astrophysical principle** from an **Earth- or Solar-System-specific
number** that cannot simply be copied onto Pandora. Include equations in LaTeX
(`$...$`, `$$...$$`) with every symbol defined and units stated.

1. **The universe's opening inventory.** Explain Big Bang nucleosynthesis: the
   conditions in the first few minutes, the neutron-to-proton ratio and its
   freeze-out, the deuterium bottleneck, and why the primordial yield is
   overwhelmingly ¹H and ⁴He with trace D, ³He and ⁷Li. Give the standard
   primordial mass fractions with uncertainties. Explain the **mass-5 and mass-8
   gaps** — the absence of stable nuclei at those masses — and why they prevent BBN
   from building carbon and beyond. State the observational pillars: the CMB
   (Planck) baryon density, primordial deuterium in high-redshift absorbers, and
   metal-poor-star lithium (including the persistent lithium problem). The reader
   must leave certain that **every carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, silicon, calcium and
   iron atom in a body was made after the Big Bang, inside stars.**
2. **Why a star shines, and what sets its fate.** Cover hydrostatic equilibrium,
   the virial temperature, the Coulomb barrier and quantum tunnelling, the Gamow
   peak, and the extreme temperature sensitivity of fusion rates. Explain the
   **p-p chain** and the **CNO cycle**, why the crossover depends on core
   temperature and hence stellar mass, and why CNO requires pre-existing metals
   (so the very first stars could not use it). Give the main-sequence
   mass–luminosity and mass–lifetime scalings, e.g. $L \propto M^{\alpha}$ with
   $\alpha \approx 3\text{–}4$ and $t_{\rm MS} \propto M/L \propto M^{-2.5}$, and
   work concrete examples: the Sun, a $0.9\,M_\odot$ star, a $25\,M_\odot$ star.
   This is what makes stellar generations and enrichment timescales intelligible.
3. **Building the elements up to iron.** Walk the advanced burning stages in a
   massive star — helium burning and the **triple-alpha** process, the
   $^{12}\mathrm{C}(\alpha,\gamma)^{16}\mathrm{O}$ reaction and why its poorly
   constrained rate governs the cosmic C/O ratio, then carbon, neon, oxygen and
   silicon burning — and the resulting onion-shell structure. Explain the
   **alpha-process/alpha-elements** and why ¹²C, ¹⁶O, ²⁰Ne, ²⁴Mg, ²⁸Si, ³²S, ⁴⁰Ca
   and ⁵⁶Ni are unusually abundant. Explain **nuclear binding energy per nucleon**,
   why it peaks near ⁵⁶Fe/⁶²Ni, and therefore why fusion cannot power a star past
   iron. Reproduce or describe the binding-energy curve and the observed cosmic
   abundance curve, and account for the odd–even (Oddo–Harkins) sawtooth.
4. **The explosive channels.** Distinguish clearly:
   - **Core-collapse supernovae** (Type II/Ib/Ic): iron core collapse, neutron
     degeneracy and bounce, neutrino energetics, the observed $\sim10^{51}$ erg of
     kinetic energy versus the far larger neutrino budget, and the yields —
     especially α-elements and oxygen. Include SN 1987A as the observational
     anchor (neutrino detection, light curve, ⁵⁶Ni→⁵⁶Co→⁵⁶Fe decay powering the
     tail).
   - **Type Ia supernovae**: white-dwarf thermonuclear runaway, why they are the
     dominant iron producers and why they lag core-collapse events, and the
     consequences for galactic [α/Fe] versus [Fe/H] trends.
   - **Neutron-star mergers**: GW170817/AT2017gfo as the direct observation of
     r-process production; the estimated ejecta mass and the identification of
     heavy elements (including strontium) in the kilonova spectrum.
   Include the pair-instability and hypernova channels only briefly, and be careful
   to present the site attributions for individual elements as an area of active
   research rather than a settled table.
5. **Neutron capture: s-process and r-process.** Explain neutron capture followed by
   beta decay; contrast **slow** capture in AGB-star helium shells (the
   ¹³C and ²²Ne neutron sources, the third dredge-up, and yields peaking at Sr, Ba,
   Pb) with **rapid** capture in neutron-rich explosive ejecta (yields at the
   r-process peaks and the actinides). Explain the role of **magic neutron numbers**
   in producing the double-peaked abundance pattern, and where the p-process and
   other minor channels fit. Then state which channel would be needed to make the
   heavy elements a body's crust contains, and how long after the Big Bang that
   channel becomes available.
6. **Stellar death, feedback and cosmic chemical evolution.** Follow enriched
   material out: AGB winds, planetary nebulae, supernova remnants, mixing into the
   interstellar medium, hot/warm/cold ISM phases, and the timescales of each.
   Define **metallicity** and the $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ notation, describe the observed
   age–metallicity relation and the galactic chemical-evolution framework, and
   explain the **G-dwarf problem** and inflow/outflow models at a conceptual level.
   Explain why planet occurrence — especially giant planets — correlates with host
   metallicity, and what that implies for a system that hosts a Jupiter-class
   planet plus a rocky moon. Report Alpha Centauri A and B's measured metallicity
   (they are metal-*rich* relative to the Sun) and stellar age estimates from the
   literature, with sources and uncertainties, and note explicitly how that helps
   or hurts a Pandora formation story.
7. **Isotopes as forensic evidence.** Explain how presolar grains in meteorites
   (SiC, graphite, nanodiamond, oxide grains) carry isotopic signatures traceable
   to individual AGB stars or supernovae; how short-lived radionuclides such as
   ²⁶Al ($t_{1/2}\approx0.72$ Myr), ⁶⁰Fe, ⁴¹Ca and ¹⁸²Hf date events in the first
   few million years of the Solar System and simultaneously provide a major early
   heat source; and how CAIs in CV chondrites furnish the canonical
   $4.567$ Gyr age of the Solar System. Explain oxygen three-isotope diagrams and
   the nucleosynthetic isotopic dichotomy (the NC/CC divide) as evidence for
   spatially distinct disk reservoirs. Explain how radiometric dating actually
   works well enough that the reader trusts the 4.567 Gyr figure without turning
   this into a geochronology tutorial.
8. **From cloud to protostar.** Cover giant molecular clouds, their masses,
   temperatures and densities; the **Jeans mass and Jeans length** with the
   equations; turbulence, magnetic support and ambipolar diffusion; what triggers
   collapse (supernova shocks, spiral-arm passage, cloud–cloud collisions); the
   conservation of angular momentum and why collapse *must* flatten into a disk;
   the protostellar accretion phases (Class 0–III); and the observational picture
   from Herschel, Spitzer, ALMA and JWST. Explain the stellar **initial mass
   function** and the fact that stars form in clusters — including the likelihood
   that the Sun formed in a cluster whose massive members injected ²⁶Al.
9. **The protoplanetary disk as a chemical factory.** Describe disk structure —
   surface density and temperature profiles, the midplane, flaring, accretion
   heating versus irradiation, viscous evolution and photoevaporative dispersal
   with observed lifetimes of a few Myr. Explain **condensation sequence** and
   equilibrium condensation temperatures; the **snow line** for water and the
   corresponding lines for CO₂, CO, N₂ and other volatiles; and how those lines
   sort the compositions of bodies with distance. Bring in the modern observational
   record: ALMA rings, gaps and spirals (HL Tau, TW Hydrae), directly detected
   protoplanets (PDS 70), and measured disk masses, dust/gas ratios and molecular
   inventories.
10. **From dust to planetesimal — including the hard parts.** Follow grain growth
    by sticking, then the two classic barriers: the **metre-size (bouncing/
    fragmentation) barrier** and the **radial-drift barrier**, and then the
    solutions — pressure-bump concentration, the **streaming instability** and
    gravitational collapse of pebble clouds. Explain **pebble accretion** versus
    planetesimal accretion, runaway and oligarchic growth, isolation mass, and the
    role of the disk's gas in damping eccentricities. Note the size distribution of
    asteroids and Kuiper-belt objects as fossil evidence.
11. **Making a giant planet.** Contrast **core accretion** (solid core, then slow
    then runaway gas envelope accretion, with the critical core mass around
    $\sim10\,M_\oplus$) against **gravitational instability** in a massive disk;
    give each model's timescale problem and observational support. Explain why
    metallicity matters for giant-planet formation, why giants preferentially form
    beyond the snow line, and how **Type I/II migration** and dynamical
    instabilities (the Grand Tack and Nice-model class of scenarios) can move a
    finished giant far from where it formed. Tie this directly to Polyphemus: a
    Jupiter-class planet in or near a habitable zone is far more easily explained as
    a migrant than as an in-situ product, and the reader should understand why.
12. **Making a moon system.** This is the step most treatments skip and the one
    Pandora most needs. Cover the three routes: a **circumplanetary disk** producing
    regular, prograde, near-coplanar satellites (the Galilean and Saturnian systems,
    including the gas-starved-disk model and the observed mass ratio of
    $\sim10^{-4}$ between satellite system and host planet); **capture**, producing
    irregular, inclined or retrograde moons (Triton, Phoebe); and **giant impact**,
    producing Earth's Moon (with the isotopic-similarity problem and the synestia
    and other variant models). Then state the quantitative tension plainly: the
    observed satellite-to-planet mass ratio implies that even a Jupiter-mass planet
    struggles to make an Earth-sized moon in its own disk. Survey the peer-reviewed
    literature on **exomoon** formation and habitability — Heller, Barnes, Kipping
    and others — for the parameter space in which a large, habitable moon is
    possible, and report what mass of host planet, what capture scenario, or what
    impact history each route requires. Include the current observational status of
    exomoon candidates (e.g. Kepler-1625b i, Kepler-1708b i) and how contested they
    are.
13. **Differentiation and the birth of an interior.** Explain the freshly accreted
    body's heat budget: accretional/gravitational binding energy, core-formation
    energy, short-lived radionuclide decay (chiefly ²⁶Al) and long-lived decay
    (⁴⁰K, ²³²Th, ²³⁵U, ²³⁸U). Explain iron–silicate separation, magma oceans,
    siderophile/lithophile/chalcophile/atmophile behaviour and the Goldschmidt
    classification, and why that partitioning strips most siderophile metals from a
    planet's crust into its core. Then use it as the analytical lever on
    unobtanium: **ore-grade concentration of a heavy phase in a crust is not what
    differentiation does by default**, so any story for unobtanium must invoke a
    specific enrichment process (late accretion, hydrothermal or magmatic
    concentration, impact delivery, or something not present in Solar-System
    geochemistry). Explain the terrestrial analogues — magmatic sulfides,
    hydrothermal veins, layered intrusions, banded iron formations, and the role of
    late accretion in the Earth's mantle's highly siderophile element budget — so
    the reader can judge which analogue could plausibly be stretched.
14. **Volatile and organic delivery.** Explain why a body forming inside the snow
    line accretes dry, and the competing sources for its water and air: local
    adsorption on grains, inward-scattered icy planetesimals, and carbonaceous
    material. Use **D/H and ¹⁵N/¹⁴N ratios** as the fingerprinting tool that
    implicates carbonaceous chondrites over comets for Earth's oceans (with the
    Rosetta/67P measurement as a key data point). Then survey the organic
    inventory of carbonaceous chondrites: Murchison's amino acids, the sugars,
    nucleobases and their isotopic evidence of extraterrestrial origin; and the
    Hayabusa2/Ryugu and OSIRIS-REx/Bennu sample-return results, which are the most
    authoritative recent data and should be cited directly. Explain
    **atmophile** behaviour and the noble gases as tracers, and specifically what
    is known about **xenon** — its cosmochemical abundance, the Earth's "missing
    xenon" and isotopic-fractionation problem, and what a *xenon-rich* atmosphere
    would require. Distinguish primary (captured nebular) atmospheres from
    secondary (outgassed) and late-delivered ones, and cover early atmospheric loss
    by hydrodynamic escape, impact erosion and stellar activity.
15. **A bounded Pandora formation model.** End with a parameter table separating:
    - values established by official Pandora canon;
    - values that can be estimated from canon plus physics (e.g. mean density from
      gravity and radius; a rock/metal/ice mix from that density; a minimum host
      mass from satellite-system mass ratios; a required disk metallicity);
    - values entirely unknown and unsafe to assume;
    - qualitative conclusions robust across a reasonable range of those unknowns.

    Then lay out **explicitly tiered formation scenarios** rather than one falsely
    precise answer — for example: (a) Pandora formed in Polyphemus's
    circumplanetary disk, which strains the observed satellite mass ratio; (b)
    Pandora formed as an independent rocky planet and was captured, which is
    dynamically demanding but relieves the mass problem; (c) Pandora is the product
    of a giant impact on a larger proto-moon. For each, state what it predicts about
    composition, water inventory, orbital regularity, sibling moons, and initial
    heat — and what observation would discriminate between them. Note which of
    Alpha Centauri's real, measured properties (high metallicity, binary
    separation and disk truncation, stellar ages) help and which hurt. Analogies
    are welcome — the moon as a second-generation object built from at least two
    prior stellar generations' ashes; the disk as a sorting machine; iron in blood
    as supernova residue — but no analogy may be reported as canon.

## Output requirements

- Cite sources inline and finish with a bibliography containing stable links,
  DOIs, book details, page numbers, film timestamps or screenplay scene references
  where available.
- Prefer primary and authoritative sources: the films and official Lightstorm/
  Avatar material for canon; peer-reviewed astrophysics, cosmochemistry and
  planetary science for the science, plus NASA, ESA, NIST, IAU, Planck/ESA data
  releases, the ALMA and JWST archives, LIGO/Virgo publications, meteoritic and
  sample-return literature (Ryugu, Bennu), and standard graduate texts. Use fan
  wikis only as leads to sources, never as canon.
- Keep **Part A (Pandora canon)** and **Part B (Earth science)** clearly separated.
  Within Part A, label each claim **official canon**, **licensed/secondary
  continuity**, **community claim**, **visual inference** or **unknown**. Within
  Part B, distinguish settled mechanism, active debate, Solar-System-specific
  empirical value, and speculative extrapolation to Pandora.
- For every important numerical claim give units, uncertainty or range, the
  measurement context, and a source. Never transfer a Solar-System number to
  Pandora without stating the assumptions that transfer requires.
- Flag contradictions, missing measurements, source circularity and the common
  misconceptions this topic attracts: that the Big Bang made the heavy elements;
  that supernovae make *all* elements heavier than iron (Type Ia iron and AGB
  s-process both matter, and the r-process has at least one non-supernova site);
  that fusion "stops at iron" because iron cannot fuse at all rather than because
  it cannot fuse *exothermically*; that the Solar System formed from a single
  supernova's debris; that planets form by dust simply clumping steadily (the
  metre barrier is real); that a habitable-zone gas giant must have formed there;
  that any gas giant automatically gets a large habitable moon; that metallicity
  means "how much metal in the rocks"; that comets delivered Earth's water (the
  isotopic evidence favours carbonaceous asteroids); and that ore deposits are
  simply where a planet "happens to have more" of an element.
- Include a concise **claim ledger** at the end with columns for claim, source,
  source tier, confidence, and permitted chapter framing (`canon`, `inference`,
  `speculation` or `real science`).
- Include a concise **chapter-use shortlist**: the 12–20 strongest facts,
  mechanisms, equations, measurements and canon gaps most worth carrying into a
  5,000–7,000-word chapter. The shortlist is guidance, not a draft; do not write
  chapter prose.
