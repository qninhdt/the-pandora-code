# Deep Research - A Planet's Bookkeeping (biogeochemical-cycles)

## Goal

This chapter teaches **two payloads at once**:

- **Pandora payload:** the Na'vi say all energy is only borrowed, and one day must be
  returned to the whole. The chapter takes that sentence literally and audits it. It
  asks what official Avatar material actually establishes about **where Pandora's
  atoms are, how fast they move, and whether the books close**: an atmosphere
  reported as carbon-dioxide-rich and hydrogen-sulfide-bearing, forests of
  extraordinary standing biomass, a root/neural network the Na'vi treat as a single
  living whole, sustained volcanism, an ocean with megafauna, and an industrial
  occupation that is physically *removing mass from the planet* and shipping it to
  another star. Four elements carry the audit - carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus,
  sulfur - and each one asks a question canon does not answer: what breaks
  Pandora's N₂ bonds, what replaces the phosphorus a fast forest strips from its
  soil, what sustains atmospheric H₂S against oxidation, and how a CO₂-rich air
  stays CO₂-rich for hundreds of millions of years without cooking or freezing the
  planet. Research must separate what an official source states from what the
  visuals merely imply, and treat canon silence as a finding rather than a hole to
  be filled with fan invention.
- **STEM payload:** real **biogeochemical cycling and planetary mass balance**. A
  biosphere is a set of **reservoirs** connected by **fluxes**, and almost every
  useful statement about a planet's chemistry is a statement about reservoir sizes,
  flux rates, and the **turnover time** $\tau = M/F$ that falls out of them. The
  chapter must deliver: the carbon cycle split into its fast biological loop and
  its slow **carbonate–silicate** loop, the temperature-dependent weathering
  feedback that acts as a planetary thermostat; the nitrogen cycle built around one
  brutally strong triple bond and the enzymes, lightning and industry that break
  it; the phosphorus cycle, which has no gas phase and therefore ties a biosphere's
  long-term fertility to rock uplift and sediment burial; and the sulfur cycle,
  which links volcanic outgassing, microbial sulfate reduction, pyrite burial and
  the redox state of an entire atmosphere. Underneath all four sits one law -
  **conservation of mass** - and one habit of mind: if a flux does not balance,
  something is either accumulating, depleting, or missing from your accounting. The
  reader should leave able to reason quantitatively about *any* planetary cycle,
  and able to tell a genuine negative feedback from a story about one.

Research must cover BOTH sides thoroughly and keep them separable. Breadth is
welcome - the author will filter aggressively.

**Scope guard (avoid overlap with neighbouring chapters).** This chapter is about
**the movement of matter through a planet: reservoirs, fluxes, turnover times, and
whether the totals balance**. Do **NOT**:

- re-run **atmospheric composition, partial pressures, hypoxia vs hypercapnia, the
  exopack, or biosignature spectroscopy** - "What's Really in the Air?" owns those.
  Atmospheric CO₂ and H₂S enter here only as *reservoirs with a source and a sink*,
  quantified in moles or gigatons, never as a respiratory-physiology lesson.
- re-run **plate tectonics mechanics, mantle convection, subduction geometry, or
  whether Pandora has plates at all** - "Pandoran Plate Tectonics" owns those. Here
  tectonics appears only as the *machine that returns buried elements*, i.e. uplift,
  weathering supply and subduction as a flux term.
- re-run **the internal heat budget, tidal dissipation, radiogenic decay, or the Io
  analogue** - "What Keeps Pandora Volcanically Alive?" owns those. Volcanism enters
  here strictly as a **degassing flux** of CO₂ and reduced sulfur, in kg/yr.
- re-run **radiometric dating, stratigraphy, molecular clocks, or reconstructing
  Pandora's chronology** - "Pandora's Deep Time" owns those. Timescales here are
  *residence times* derived from reservoir/flux ratios, not absolute ages.
- re-run **photosynthetic pigments, absorption spectra, stomata, phloem transport,
  or how an individual leaf fixes carbon** - "How Does a Pandoran Plant Feed?" owns
  those. Photosynthesis enters here only as an aggregate flux (gross and net primary
  production) at ecosystem and planetary scale.
- re-run **alternative biochemistries, chirality, silicon life, solvents, or the
  definition of life** - "What Is Pandoran Life Made Of?" owns those. Assume carbon,
  water and a CHNOPS-like toolkit and get on with the accounting.
- re-run **mycorrhizal networks, the wood-wide web, soil food webs, decomposer
  guilds, or microbiome/holobiont theory** - those belong to "The Wood Wide Web" and
  "Pandora's Smallest Things". Decomposers appear here as a **remineralisation flux
  rate**, not as an ecology or a network story.
- re-run **Eywa as distributed cognition, information theory, signal bandwidth, or
  the weak-vs-strong Gaia debate as a philosophical position** - Part V, and
  especially "Gaia After Eywa", owns that argument. This chapter may set up *only*
  the mechanical precondition: what a control loop needs (sensor, setpoint,
  actuator, negative gain, delay) and whether biogeochemical feedbacks on Earth
  supply it without any coordinating mind. Do not adjudicate Eywa.
- re-run **unobtanium economics, EROI, interstellar shipping, or the ethics of
  extraction** - Parts IX and X own those. RDA extraction enters here as one term
  only: a mass **export flux** off the planet, and what an open cycle does to a
  budget that was previously closed.
- turn the chapter into **a general environmental-science survey or a climate-change
  editorial**. Anthropogenic perturbation of Earth's cycles is the best available
  natural experiment for measuring fluxes and feedbacks - use it as evidence, not as
  advocacy.

## Part A - Pandora canon

Research what official Avatar material establishes about **the stocks and flows of
matter on Pandora**: what the atmosphere and oceans contain and in what amounts, how
much living matter the planet carries, how fast material is produced and recycled,
what volcanism and the seas put in and take out, what the Na'vi say about borrowing
and returning, and what the RDA physically removes. Be specific, cite sources, and
distinguish official canon (the films *Avatar* (2009), *Avatar: The Way of Water*
(2022), *Avatar: Fire and Ash* (2025) where released material allows, published
screenplays, *James Cameron's Avatar: An Activist Survival Guide* by Wilhelm &
Mathison, *The World of Avatar: A Visual Exploration*, *The Wild Life of Pandora* by
Dinda, *The Art of Avatar* volumes, Pandorapedia and other Cameron/Lightstorm-
sanctioned material) from licensed game/comic continuity (*Avatar: Frontiers of
Pandora*, tie-in comics, RPG guides) and from fan-wiki/community extrapolation. State
the exact official status of any licensed secondary source rather than silently
promoting it.

1. **The atmospheric inventory, as numbers.** Collect every official statement of
   Pandora's atmospheric composition and physical state: surface pressure relative to
   Earth's, the reported fraction or partial pressure of N₂, O₂, CO₂, H₂S, methane,
   xenon, and any other named gas, plus temperature and scale height if given. Report
   the *original* figures with their source and note where different official sources
   disagree. Then convert: given Pandora's stated radius, mass or surface gravity, how
   many kilograms or moles of each gas does the quoted composition imply? A total
   atmospheric mass and per-species reservoir size is the single most valuable number
   this research can hand the chapter, because every flux estimate downstream is
   compared against it. Flag clearly which of these numbers are canonical and which
   are your own arithmetic from canonical inputs.

2. **How much living matter is there?** Gather any official claim about Pandoran
   biomass, forest productivity, growth rates, tree size and density, canopy height,
   root-mass, ocean productivity, or "the richest biosphere known" style assertions.
   Look for stated dimensions of Hometree and other named megaflora, described forest
   density, and any number for how fast Pandoran vegetation grows or regrows. Where
   canon gives dimensions but not mass, say so - the chapter can estimate, but it
   must know it is estimating. Also collect what canon says about the *ocean* side:
   reef extent, plankton-equivalents, tulkun population scale and body mass.

3. **Volcanism and outgassing as a source term.** What official material says about
   the rate, extent and persistence of Pandoran volcanism, ash deposition, geothermal
   or hydrothermal activity, sulfurous emissions, and the Mangkwan clan's eruption.
   Is there any canonical statement of how *much* material erupts or how frequently?
   Is atmospheric H₂S ever explicitly attributed to volcanism, or is that a community
   inference? Distinguish a described event from a stated rate.

4. **Where the dead things go - the return flux.** Every canonical or clearly
   image-supported observation about decay, litter, rot, scavenging, soil, mud, peat,
   ash chemistry, and the fate of large carcasses and fallen megaflora. Does canon
   ever state that dead matter *becomes* new growth, or that nutrients cycle, in
   mechanistic rather than spiritual terms? Record the Na'vi framing separately from
   any material claim: quote the "all energy is only borrowed" line and any related
   dialogue or ritual text verbatim, with film and scene attribution, and note
   whether characters ever describe it as physics rather than faith.

5. **Does canon ever mention nitrogen fixation, fertility, or limiting nutrients?**
   Search hard and report honestly, including a null result. Look for any reference to
   soil fertility, nutrient-poor or nutrient-rich ground, fertiliser, nitrogen,
   phosphates, guano-equivalents, ash as fertiliser, or crop/garden cultivation by any
   clan. Check whether the root network is ever said to move *nutrients or matter*
   rather than only signals and memory - these are routinely conflated by fans and the
   distinction is load-bearing for this chapter.

6. **The ocean's role in the budget.** What canon establishes about Pandora's seas as
   a chemical reservoir: salinity or composition if ever stated, dissolved gases,
   upwelling or nutrient-rich water, reef mineralogy (is anything described as
   calcium-carbonate-like?), sediment, and whether the ocean is presented as a sink
   for anything. Keep this to *chemistry and mass*, not physical oceanography.

7. **The RDA as an open hole in a closed system.** Assemble what official sources say
   about the physical quantities the RDA removes and adds: unobtanium tonnage mined,
   shipped mass per ISV voyage, the scale of clear-cutting and open-pit operations,
   defoliants, ash and combustion products from burning forest, the Bridgehead/Hell's
   Gate footprint, sea-floor or ocean industry in the later films, and any figure for
   ore grade or output. Also: what does canon say about material the RDA *imports* to
   Pandora and about waste it leaves behind? A mass-balance chapter needs the export
   and import terms even if only order-of-magnitude.

8. **Timescales canon commits to.** Any official statement bearing on how long
   Pandora's biosphere, atmosphere or forests have existed in their present state, how
   old named organisms or Hometrees are, how fast burned or logged areas recover, and
   how long the RDA has been operating. These constrain whether the quantities in A1–A7
   represent a steady state or a system in transition.

9. **Where canon is silent, vague, or self-contradictory - ranked.** Give the gaps
   their own section, ordered by how much they matter to a mass-balance audit. Expect
   at minimum: no stated nitrogen-fixation mechanism; no phosphorus source or soil
   chemistry; no quantified volcanic degassing rate; no explanation for how a
   CO₂-rich atmosphere is maintained rather than drawn down by weathering and burial;
   no stated sulfur sink; no biomass or productivity figures; conflicting atmospheric
   percentages between sources; and the unresolved question of whether the planetary
   network *regulates* chemistry or merely results from it. For each gap, state
   precisely what an official source would have to say to close it. These gaps are
   where the chapter does its real science.

## Part B - Earth-science literature (biogeochemical cycles and planetary mass balance)

The real science, from credible and where possible primary sources, explained at a
level a curious non-specialist can follow but with the actual numbers intact.

1. **The box-model grammar of every cycle.** Define **reservoir** (or pool, in mass
   units), **flux** (mass per unit time), **source**, **sink**, **steady state**, and
   **turnover / residence time** $\tau = M/F$. Show how $\tau$ is computed and what it
   means physically, with worked Earth examples spanning many orders of magnitude
   (e.g. atmospheric water vapour ~9 days, atmospheric CO₂ ~4–5 years for a single
   molecule's exchange versus far longer for a *perturbation* to relax - and explain
   why those two numbers are different, since conflating them is one of the most
   common errors in popular writing). Cover **first-order kinetics** and exponential
   relaxation, why a small reservoir with a large flux responds fast while a large
   reservoir with a small flux buffers, and how **coupled boxes** produce multiple
   relaxation timescales. Distinguish **negative feedback** (stabilising, with gain
   and delay) from **positive feedback**, and give the standard control-theory
   vocabulary. Reference the standard texts (e.g. **Schlesinger & Bernhardt**,
   *Biogeochemistry*; **Sarmiento & Gruber**, *Ocean Biogeochemical Dynamics*;
   **Berner**, *The Phanerozoic Carbon Cycle*; **Kump, Kasting & Crane**, *The Earth
   System*).

2. **The carbon cycle, fast loop.** Quantify the modern Earth reservoirs in gigatons
   of carbon (GtC): atmosphere, surface and deep ocean, dissolved inorganic carbon,
   terrestrial vegetation, soils and permafrost, marine biota, fossil fuels,
   sedimentary carbonate. Then the fluxes: **gross primary production**, **net primary
   production**, autotrophic and heterotrophic **respiration**, net ecosystem
   exchange, air–sea exchange in both directions, riverine transport, and burial.
   Explain why the *gross* two-way exchanges are an order of magnitude larger than the
   net imbalance, and why that makes the net imbalance hard to measure but decisive.
   Include the carbonate system in seawater: CO₂/H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻/CO₃²⁻ speciation, the
   **Revelle factor** and why the ocean's buffering capacity is finite, the
   **solubility pump** and the **biological pump**, the **carbonate compensation
   depth**, and what fraction of exported organic carbon actually reaches the sediment.

3. **The carbon cycle, slow loop - the planetary thermostat.** This is the chapter's
   most important single mechanism, so treat it rigorously. Give the
   **carbonate–silicate (Urey) cycle** with the actual reactions: silicate weathering
   consuming CO₂ ($\mathrm{CaSiO_3 + CO_2 \rightarrow CaCO_3 + SiO_2}$ in its
   idealised Urey form, plus the realistic two-step version via carbonic acid and
   bicarbonate transport to the sea), marine carbonate precipitation, subduction, and
   metamorphic/volcanic return. Then the **feedback**: how weathering rate depends on
   temperature, precipitation, CO₂ partial pressure, physical erosion and exposed
   fresh rock area; the empirical and theoretical scaling laws used
   (**Walker, Hays & Kasting 1981**; **Berner–Lasaga–Garrels BLAG**; **GEOCARB** and
   **GEOCARBSULF**; the **Arrhenius** temperature dependence and typical activation
   energies; the **runoff** and **pCO₂** exponents actually fitted). Quantify: how long
   does this thermostat take to act, i.e. what is the response time of the
   silicate-weathering feedback (order $10^5$–$10^6$ years) and therefore what kinds of
   perturbation it can and cannot absorb. Cover the **faint young Sun problem** as the
   canonical evidence that the thermostat has been operating, the **kinetic vs
   supply-limited** weathering regimes, the **uplift/erosion** control (Raymo &
   Ruddiman) and the **snowball Earth** episodes as the thermostat's failure-and-
   recovery mode. State honestly where the field is unsettled: the strength of the
   feedback, the role of land plants in accelerating weathering, and whether
   weathering is thermodynamically or kinetically limited at high pCO₂.

4. **The nitrogen cycle and the strongest bond in the air.** Start with the physical
   fact: the N≡N triple bond at roughly $941\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, which makes a
   78%-N₂ atmosphere effectively inert and makes nitrogen the textbook case of an
   element that is abundant *and* limiting. Then the routes that break it:
   **biological nitrogen fixation** by nitrogenase (its molybdenum–iron cofactor,
   extreme oxygen sensitivity, the ATP and reductant cost per N₂ reduced, and why that
   cost governs where fixers can afford to live); **lightning** fixation (give the
   estimated global rate in Tg N/yr and note the wide uncertainty); and the
   **Haber–Bosch** process (conditions, energy cost, and the fact that industrial
   fixation now rivals or exceeds all natural terrestrial fixation - one of the
   cleanest illustrations of a human-perturbed global cycle). Then the rest of the
   loop: **assimilation**, **ammonification**, **nitrification**
   ($\mathrm{NH_4^+ \rightarrow NO_2^- \rightarrow NO_3^-}$, including comammox),
   **denitrification** back to N₂, **anammox**, and the leak terms N₂O and NO with
   their climate and ozone consequences. Give modern reservoir sizes (atmospheric N₂,
   soil organic N, ocean nitrate, biomass N) and flux magnitudes in Tg N/yr, plus the
   **redfield-ratio** link to phosphorus and the long-running argument over whether
   marine N is ultimately regulated by P availability through fixation–denitrification
   balance.

5. **The phosphorus cycle - no gas phase, and what that costs.** Explain why the
   absence of a stable volatile P species changes everything: P enters the biosphere
   only by **chemical weathering of apatite and other phosphate minerals**, moves by
   rivers and dust, and leaves only by **sedimentation and burial**, so the cycle is
   inescapably tied to tectonic uplift and ocean sedimentation. Give the reservoirs
   and the very long oceanic residence time (order $10^4$ years, with the sedimentary
   reservoir far larger), the fluxes in Tg P/yr, and the mechanisms that recycle P
   locally at high efficiency (rapid microbial remineralisation, mycorrhizal and root
   phosphatase strategies, extreme tightness of P cycling in old tropical soils and in
   canopy/epiphyte communities). Cover **Liebig's law of the minimum** and the
   evidence for N versus P limitation in different ecosystems, marine versus
   freshwater; the **Redfield ratio** 106C:16N:1P, what it is and how much it actually
   varies; **iron** as the other classic limiter (HNLC regions, iron fertilisation
   experiments and their disappointing carbon-export results); and the modern human
   perturbation - mined phosphate rock, eutrophication, dead zones, and the finite-
   reserves debate. Finish with why P is the element that makes a *geologically dead*
   planet eventually infertile.

6. **The sulfur cycle and the redox state of an atmosphere.** This is where Pandora
   diverges most sharply, so cover it in unusual depth. Give the oxidation states
   sulfur moves through and the main species: H₂S, SO₂, SO₄²⁻, elemental S, pyrite
   FeS₂, dimethyl sulfide. Then the fluxes: **volcanic and hydrothermal outgassing**
   (with modern Earth estimates in Tg S/yr and the SO₂:H₂S ratio's dependence on
   magma oxidation state, pressure and temperature), **microbial sulfate reduction**
   and the sedimentary pyrite sink, **DMS** production by marine plankton and the
   CLAW-hypothesis cloud-albedo feedback (and the substantial criticism it has since
   received), **sulfide oxidation** both abiotic and microbial, sea-salt sulfate, and
   the anthropogenic term. Crucially: quantify **the atmospheric lifetime of H₂S in an
   oxygen-bearing atmosphere** - the reaction with OH and O₂, the resulting lifetime
   of order *hours to a few days*, and therefore how enormous a continuous source flux
   would be needed to hold H₂S at any measurable steady-state mixing ratio in an
   oxidising atmosphere. Then give the comparative planetology: **Venus** (SO₂, sulfuric
   acid clouds, and its own sulfur redox cycle), **Io** (SO₂ frost and volcanic
   plumes), **Archean Earth** (mass-independent sulfur isotope fractionation as the
   fingerprint of a low-O₂ atmosphere, and the Great Oxidation Event), and volcanic
   plume chemistry generally. Explain how **sulfur isotopes** ($\delta^{34}$S, $\Delta^{33}$S)
   are used as a diagnostic tool for reconstructing past atmospheric redox - a nice
   demonstration that a cycle leaves a readable record.

7. **Oxygen, and why cycles must be coupled.** Explain why the four cycles cannot be
   audited separately: the long-term O₂ budget is set by **organic carbon and pyrite
   burial** versus **weathering of reduced phases**, which welds carbon to sulfur; the
   N:P stoichiometry of marine production welds nitrogen to phosphorus; and biological
   demand for all four is set by roughly fixed elemental ratios. Cover the standard
   long-term redox accounting (Garrels & Perry; the **GEOCARBSULF** family; Berner's
   O₂ reconstructions and the Carboniferous high-O₂ episode), the concept of
   **oxidative weathering** as a negative feedback on atmospheric O₂, and the
   **stoichiometric constraint** approach in general. This section is what lets the
   chapter argue that you cannot change one Pandoran cycle without paying for it in
   another.

8. **Measuring fluxes for real - how anyone knows any of these numbers.** Survey the
   actual methods, because a mass-balance chapter is only credible if the reader knows
   the numbers were measured rather than asserted: **eddy covariance** flux towers and
   FLUXNET, **isotopic tracers** ($^{13}$C, $^{14}$C bomb-spike, $^{15}$N, $^{18}$O,
   $^{34}$S) and what each traces, the **Keeling curve** and the atmospheric O₂/N₂
   ratio method for partitioning land versus ocean carbon uptake, **sediment traps**
   and thorium-234 for export production, **satellite ocean-colour** productivity
   estimates, **atmospheric inversions**, catchment-scale **weathering flux** budgets
   from river chemistry, and **mesocosm and whole-ecosystem manipulation** experiments
   (FACE experiments for CO₂; nutrient-addition experiments such as Hubbard Brook,
   Nutrient Network, and the classic lake-eutrophication whole-lake experiments -
   **Schindler's Lake 226** is an excellent single case study). For each method, note
   what it measures well and its main uncertainty. Also cover how **global budgets are
   closed** and what "residual sink" means when they do not close - the missing-carbon-
   sink history is a perfect illustration of mass balance used as an inferential tool.

9. **Open versus closed systems, and what leaves a planet.** Cover the physics of
   planetary-scale mass loss and gain: **atmospheric escape** (Jeans/thermal,
   hydrodynamic, non-thermal and impact-driven), the **cosmic-ray and micrometeorite**
   input, and why for most elements a planet is effectively closed on biological
   timescales - which is exactly what makes recycling mandatory rather than virtuous.
   Then the useful contrast cases: closed-system experiments and their failures -
   **Biosphere 2** (the oxygen decline caused by soil respiration plus CO₂ absorption
   into unsealed concrete, a superb worked example of an unaudited sink), **BIOS-3**,
   and NASA/ESA closed-loop life support (**ECLSS**, water and CO₂ recovery
   fractions, **MELiSSA**) with their actual closure percentages. These give the
   chapter a rigorous way to talk about what "closing the books" costs in practice.

10. **Human perturbation as the natural experiment.** Use the anthropogenic
    disturbance of each cycle purely as measurement, not advocacy: the carbon budget
    (emissions, airborne fraction, land and ocean sinks, ocean acidification's ΔpH),
    the nitrogen cascade and Haber–Bosch's doubling of reactive N, phosphate mining
    and eutrophication, and the rise-and-fall of sulfate aerosol emissions with its
    detectable climate signature. Include the **planetary boundaries** framework
    (Rockström, Steffen and later revisions) as a quantitative statement about cycle
    perturbation, along with its main scientific criticisms. The teaching value here is
    that we know these cycles' sensitivities *because* we pushed them.

11. **Do biogeochemical feedbacks amount to regulation?** Set up - carefully and
    without adjudicating Eywa - the mechanical question the chapter needs. What does a
    genuine regulatory loop require: a measured variable, an effective setpoint, a
    negative-gain response, an actuator, and a delay short enough relative to the
    disturbance. Show how silicate weathering meets that description with *no
    coordination whatsoever*, then contrast with cases where a proposed biological
    thermostat is weak or disputed (CLAW/DMS), and with **Daisyworld** as an existence
    proof that homeostasis can emerge from competition alone. Present the **Gaia**
    literature only at this mechanical level (Lovelock & Margulis's original claim,
    Watson & Lovelock's Daisyworld, and the standard objections from **Kirchner** and
    **Doolittle** about testability and the impossibility of planet-level selection),
    and explicitly leave the verdict to the later chapter. Also cover the honest
    counter-case: **evolutionary suicide** and destabilising biological feedbacks - the
    Great Oxidation Event, snowball glaciations plausibly triggered by biology, and
    end-Permian sulfidic ocean scenarios - because a biosphere that regulated reliably
    would not have those events in its record.

12. **Analogies connecting the Earth science back to Pandora - and where each breaks.**
    Evaluate explicitly, with the failure mode of each stated:
    - **Pandora's CO₂-rich air ≈ Archean/early Earth or a pre-thermostat state:** good
      for explaining what high pCO₂ does to weathering rate and climate; breaks because
      Pandora hosts a mature oxygen-bearing biosphere at the same time, which on Earth
      coincided with much lower pCO₂. What sustained source or throttled sink could
      hold both at once?
    - **Atmospheric H₂S ≈ volcanic plume chemistry / Venus / Io:** good for the source
      side; breaks hard on the sink side, because H₂S oxidises in hours to days in any
      O₂-bearing air, so the required volcanic flux may be implausibly large. Work the
      number rather than asserting the conclusion.
    - **Fast-growing forest on old soil ≈ Amazonian nutrient economy:** strong analogy
      for extremely tight P recycling, canopy nutrient capture, and the dust/ash
      subsidy; breaks if Pandora's productivity is far above tropical Earth's, since P
      supply, not carbon, would then bind.
    - **Volcanism as fertiliser ≈ Hawaiian/Icelandic tephra soils and ash-fall
      fertilisation events:** excellent for connecting the volcanism chapter to the
      nutrient budget without re-teaching it.
    - **The root network as a nutrient distributor ≈ mycorrhizal transfer:** flag the
      well-documented gap between signal transfer and bulk nutrient transfer, and note
      that the network chapters own the mechanism.
    - **RDA extraction ≈ an open cycle / a mine tailings + biomass export problem:**
      rigorous and quantifiable; state clearly that unobtanium export is a tiny mass
      term compared with deforestation's carbon and nutrient losses, if the numbers
      bear that out.
    - **"All energy is only borrowed" ≈ conservation of mass:** the chapter's spine.
      Note precisely where the metaphor is exactly right (matter is conserved and
      recycled) and where it quietly fails (**energy** is *not* recycled; it degrades
      and is radiated away, so a biosphere is materially near-closed but
      thermodynamically wide open). That distinction is the single most valuable idea
      in the chapter - please supply the clean physics of it: solar input, entropy
      export, and why "closed loop" applies to atoms but never to energy.
