# Deep Research - Continents, Oceans, Climate (continents-oceans-climate)

## Goal

This chapter teaches **two payloads at once**:

- **Pandora payload:** Pandora's **surface geography and climate as a whole
  planet (moon)** - its continents and landmasses, the great **Eastern Sea**, the
  distribution of mountains (including the Hallelujah / flux-vortex region),
  rivers, rainforests, and the named regions where the films and games are set.
  What canon establishes about Pandora's **climate zones and biomes** (equatorial
  rainforest, the reef/ocean world of the second film, any polar or arid regions),
  its weather (storms, the dense humid air, day-length and eclipses from
  Polyphemus), and how being a **moon of a gas giant in the Alpha Centauri A
  system** shapes its seasons, tides, and heat budget.
- **STEM payload:** the real **comparative climatology** that explains why a
  planet's surface looks the way it does - how **incoming stellar energy + axial
  tilt + rotation** drive **atmospheric general circulation**, especially the
  **Hadley / Ferrel / polar cells** and the resulting bands of wet and dry climate
  (why rainforests sit near the equator and deserts near ~30 degrees latitude);
  how **ocean circulation** and land-sea distribution redistribute heat and
  moisture; and how climate, geology, and water together sort the surface into
  **biomes**. The reader should come away able to predict, from a few orbital and
  atmospheric parameters, roughly *where* a world's forests, deserts, and oceans
  would fall - and then test Pandora against that template.

Research must cover BOTH sides thoroughly and keep them separable. Breadth is
welcome - the author will filter aggressively.

## Part A - Pandora canon

What official Avatar material establishes about Pandora's geography, oceans, and
climate. Be specific and cite sources; distinguish **official canon** (the films

3. **Climate zones and biomes:** every canonical statement about Pandora's

4. **Orbital / astronomical drivers of climate:** what canon establishes about
   Pandora as a **moon of the gas giant Polyphemus** orbiting **Alpha Centauri A** -

5. **Atmosphere's role in climate:** canon describes a **dense, humid atmosphere**

## Part B - Earth-science literature (comparative climatology, circulation, biomes)

The real climate science, explained from credible/primary sources, at a level a
curious non-specialist can follow:

1. **The planetary energy budget:** how a world's climate starts from **incoming
   stellar radiation vs outgoing infrared**, the role of **albedo** and the
   **greenhouse effect**, and why the **equator receives more energy per unit area
   than the poles** (the cosine-of-latitude / solar incidence-angle argument).
   This latitudinal heating gradient is the engine for everything that follows.
2. **Atmospheric general circulation - the three-cell model:** the **Hadley
   cell**, **Ferrel cell**, and **polar cell**; why warm air rises at the equator
   (the **ITCZ**, heavy rainfall) and descends near **~30 degrees latitude**
   (subtropical highs, the world's great **deserts**); the resulting surface wind
   belts (**trade winds**, westerlies). Explain with numbers and mechanisms a
   layperson can grasp.
3. **Rotation and the Coriolis effect:** how a planet's **rotation rate** deflects
   moving air and water, why this breaks a single equator-to-pole cell into
   multiple cells, and how **faster or slower rotation** would change the number
   and width of circulation cells (relevant if Pandora's day length differs from
   Earth's). Mention tidally locked / slow-rotator climate regimes as the extreme
   case.
4. **Axial tilt, seasons, and climate bands:** how **obliquity** creates seasons
   and sets the latitudes of the tropics and polar circles; what high vs low (or
   zero) axial tilt does to seasonality and to the existence of ice caps. Include
   how a **moon orbiting a giant planet** experiences seasons and **eclipses**, and
   how tidal heating/illumination from the primary can matter (use the real
   science on moons and exomoon climate where available).
5. **Ocean circulation and heat transport:** how oceans store and move heat -
   **wind-driven surface gyres**, **upwelling**, and the **thermohaline
   circulation**; how oceans moderate coastal climate and drive rainfall; the
   effect of **land-sea distribution** (continentality vs maritime climate) on a
   planet's surface. Note how a mostly-ocean world (echoing the second film) would
   differ climatically from a continental one.
6. **From climate to biomes:** how temperature + precipitation sort the surface
   into **biomes** - the classic **Whittaker biome diagram** (rainforest,
   savanna, desert, temperate forest, taiga, tundra) and **Koppen climate
   classification**. Why **tropical rainforests cluster near the equator** (the
   ascending wet branch of the Hadley cell) and deserts near the subtropics -
   exactly the band logic that should predict where Pandora's rainforests sit.
7. **Comparative planetology:** brief, concrete comparisons of how circulation and
   climate differ on **Earth, Mars, Venus, and Titan** (a thick-atmosphere moon!),
   and what slow rotation or a very thick/dense atmosphere does to heat transport
   and climate banding. Use this to frame Pandora's dense atmosphere and moon
   status as a real comparative-climatology case.
8. Good **analogies** connecting the Earth science back to Pandora - e.g. using
   the Hadley-cell band logic to predict that Pandora's equatorial rainforests and
   any subtropical dry zones should fall in specific latitude bands; how Pandora's
   **denser atmosphere** would make heat transport more efficient and possibly
   shrink temperature contrasts; and how a mostly-rainforest-and-ocean world is a
   plausible (or implausible) outcome of its orbital + atmospheric parameters.
