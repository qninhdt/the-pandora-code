# Deep Research - A Crust That Moves (pandoran-plate-tectonics)

## Goal

This chapter teaches **two payloads at once**:

- **Pandora payload:** whether Pandora's crust **moves**. Canon shows four things
  that on Earth are all products of one machine: **mountain ranges** (the Hallelujah
  range and the highlands beneath it, plus whatever collisional or volcanic ranges
  official material names), **deep ocean basins** distinct from continental shelves,
  a **volcanic province** violent enough to bury a clan's homeland, and
  **ore bodies** — unobtanium above all — concentrated richly enough that a
  corporation will cross four light-years to dig them out. On Earth every one of
  those four is downstream of **plate tectonics**. Yet no official Avatar source
  ever says Pandora has plates. This chapter asks whether the inference is *forced*,
  *permitted*, or *wrong* — and it must be honest that the answer is not obviously
  yes. The research must therefore assemble the circumstantial geological evidence
  canon actually provides (landforms, basins, volcanism, ore, seismicity, any stated
  interior structure) and separate it cleanly from what fans and visual inference
  have back-filled. The chapter's central tension is real and should be sharpened,
  not smoothed: **Io is tidally heated much as Pandora is inferred to be, and Io has
  no plates.** Io vents essentially its entire heat budget through volcanism and
  resurfaces itself without ever rifting or subducting. A world with both a strong
  tidal heat budget *and* collisional mountain belts would be solving a problem no
  body in the Solar System solves that way. The research must supply what is needed
  to state that tension quantitatively and to mark plainly where inference ends and
  guessing begins.
- **STEM payload:** how humanity learned that **a planet's interior is knowable and
  its surface is mobile** — the two greatest inferential achievements in solid-Earth
  science, both made without anyone ever reaching the places being described.
  First, **seismology as a remote-sensing instrument**: P and S waves, refraction
  and reflection, travel-time curves, the **S-wave shadow zone** that proves the
  outer core is liquid, the **Mohorovičić discontinuity**, the Gutenberg and
  Lehmann discontinuities, PKIKP and the solid inner core, and modern seismic
  tomography. Second, the **engine**: heat transport by conduction versus solid-state
  **mantle convection**, the **Rayleigh number** and the criterion for the onset of
  convection, viscosity and its temperature dependence, the Nusselt–Rayleigh scaling
  that sets how efficiently a mantle can shed heat, and the role of **plate bending,
  slab pull and ridge push** as the actual force balance driving plate motion.
  Third, the **regime question** — the single most transferable idea in the chapter:
  a rocky body does not "have or lack" tectonics, it settles into one of a small
  number of convective regimes — **mobile-lid / plate tectonics** (Earth),
  **stagnant lid** (Venus, Mars, the Moon), **sluggish lid**, or **heat-pipe**
  (Io) — and which regime it lands in is set by computable things: lithospheric
  yield strength, the ratio of convective stress to that strength, surface
  temperature, water content and the presence of weak faults. The reader should
  leave able to look at any rocky world's heat flow, size and surface age and reason
  about which regime it must be in, and to explain why Earth is, on present evidence,
  the *only* body in the Solar System in the mobile-lid regime.

Research must cover BOTH sides thoroughly and keep them separable. Breadth is
welcome — the author will filter aggressively.

**Scope guard (avoid overlap with neighbouring chapters):** This chapter is about
**the solid interior and the mobility of the crust** — how we know what is inside a
rocky body, how its heat moves, which convective regime it occupies, and what
landforms and ore bodies each regime produces. It begins with a body that already
exists as a differentiated moon and ends at the surface expression of its interior
dynamics. Do **NOT**:

- re-derive **nucleosynthesis, disk accretion, moon formation or differentiation
  itself** — “From Stardust to Pandora” owns origins. Core/mantle/crust separation
  is that chapter's; what the resulting layers *do afterwards* is this one's.
- re-run the **tidal heating budget**: eccentricity, resonance pumping, Love numbers,
  the quality factor $Q$, the induction-heating correction, or the Mangkwan/Ashlands
  eruption as a catastrophe narrative — “What keeps Pandora volcanically alive?”
  already teaches all of that and concluded that tidal dissipation delivers of order
  a few hundred terawatts to Pandora's interior. **Take that heat budget as a given
  input** and ask the next question: what does a mantle *do* with that much heat, and
  which convective regime does it force? The novel material here is heat *transport*
  and *regime selection*, not heat *production*.
- teach **superconductivity, Cooper pairs, the Meissner effect or flux pinning**, or
  re-explain why the Hallelujah Mountains levitate — “Floating mountains, unobtanium
  and the Flux Devil” owns unobtanium's physics. Here unobtanium matters only as an
  **ore-genesis problem**: what crustal process concentrates any exotic phase to
  minable grade, and whether that process requires a mobile lid.
- reconstruct Pandora's **geological chronology**, teach radiometric dating practice,
  stratigraphy, crater counting or molecular clocks — that is “Pandora's deep time.”
  Surface age appears here only as the *diagnostic* of a regime (crater density as a
  resurfacing-rate proxy), not as a dating tutorial.
- teach the **carbonate–silicate thermostat** as a climate mechanism — “Where is
  Pandora?” and “A real living planet” own it. It may appear here only as one of the
  *consequences* of subduction, in a sentence, pointing forward.
- run the **phosphorus cycle or nutrient limitation** — “A planet's bookkeeping”
  owns biogeochemical cycles. Rock weathering as a nutrient source may be named as a
  consequence of uplift, not developed.
- narrate **RDA mining operations, economics or colonial history**. Ore genesis is
  geology here, not politics.

   *infers from a matte painting*. Note explicitly that the floating mountains are a

3. **Volcanism and its spatial pattern.** Building on the volcanism chapter but
   asking a *different* question: not *where does the heat come from* but *where do
   the volcanoes sit*. Is there any canon on the **distribution** of volcanic
   features — linear chains, arcs, rifts, isolated shields, flood-basalt provinces,
   calderas? Distribution is the diagnostic: arcs imply subduction, rifts imply
   extension, isolated shields imply hotspots over a stagnant lid, and pervasive
   ubiquitous volcanism implies a heat-pipe. Record what canon supports and where it
   is simply silent. Include the Ashlands' setting and any statement about the
   volcano that destroyed the Mangkwan homeland — its type (shield, stratovolcano,

4. **Earthquakes, faults and seismicity.** Is there *any* canonical reference to

5. **Unobtanium and ore bodies as geological objects.** Everything official on where

   Also record any other economically named resource. Then state the geological
   problem: **what crustal process concentrates a phase to minable grade**, and
   whether every terrestrial analogue for that process (magmatic, hydrothermal,
   orogenic, sedimentary) requires a mobile lid, active fluid circulation or
   long-lived crustal recycling.
6. **Interior structure, if canon says anything at all.** Any statement or strong

   magnetic field** versus an induced one. A self-generated dynamo is significant
   evidence about the interior: it requires a convecting, electrically conducting

   Trace the canonical magnetic-field claims carefully and say what they license.
7. **Water, and the fact that Pandora is wet.** Record canon on surface water,

8. **Bulk parameters relevant to the interior.** Repeat only what is needed as

## Part B - Earth-science literature (seismology, mantle convection, tectonic regimes, ore genesis)

Explain the real science from credible and preferably primary sources at a level a
curious non-specialist can follow. Use named observations, instruments, experiments,
quantitative ranges and dates wherever they illuminate a mechanism. Throughout,
distinguish a **general physical principle** from an **Earth-specific empirical
number** that cannot simply be copied onto another world. Include equations in LaTeX
(`$...$`, `$$...$$`) with every symbol defined and units stated.

1. **The problem of seeing inside a planet.** Establish the scale of the inferential
   feat: the deepest borehole ever drilled (Kola Superdeep, ~12.26 km) reaches a
   fraction of a percent of Earth's radius, so essentially everything known about the
   interior is **remotely inferred**. List the independent constraint families and
   what each one alone can and cannot fix: total mass and mean density from gravity;
   the **moment of inertia factor** $C/MR^2$ as a measure of internal concentration;
   **seismology**; the magnetic field as evidence of a conducting fluid layer;
   surface heat flow; high-pressure mineral physics experiments (diamond anvil cells,
   shock experiments); mantle xenoliths and ophiolites as direct rock samples; and
   **geoneutrinos**. Emphasise that the interior is over-determined by several
   independent methods that agree — that is why the picture is trusted.
2. **Seismic waves as the primary instrument.** Explain body waves versus surface
   waves; **P (compressional)** and **S (shear)** waves; the elastic moduli that set
   their speeds,
   $$v_P=\sqrt{\frac{K+\tfrac{4}{3}\mu}{\rho}},\qquad v_S=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}}$$
   with $K$ the bulk modulus, $\mu$ the shear modulus and $\rho$ density — and the
   single most consequential fact in the chapter: **a fluid has $\mu=0$, so a fluid
   cannot transmit S waves at all.** Then cover refraction and reflection at
   interfaces, Snell's law in a radially stratified body, ray paths that curve
   because velocity increases with depth, travel-time curves, the standard phase
   naming convention (P, S, PP, PcP, PKP, **PKIKP**, ScS), and how an array of
   stations turns arrival times into a depth–velocity profile. Explain the
   **normal-mode / free-oscillation** method as the complementary long-period
   constraint.
3. **The great seismological discoveries, as case studies in inference.** Tell each
   as an argument with a date, a person and a data set, because the chapter reuses
   them as a template for reasoning about an unmeasured world:
   - **Mohorovičić (1909)**, the Kupa Valley earthquake, and the double set of
     arrivals that revealed the **crust–mantle boundary (the Moho)**; typical Moho
     depth under continents versus oceans and the velocity jump across it.
   - **Oldham and Gutenberg (1906–1914)** and the **core–mantle boundary** at
     ~2,890 km; the **P-wave shadow zone** between roughly 103° and 143° epicentral
     distance, and above all the **S-wave shadow** beyond ~103° that proves the outer
     core is **liquid**. Explain carefully why the *absence* of a signal is the
     evidence, and how a comparable observation on any other world would settle its
     core state.
   - **Inge Lehmann (1936)** and the faint precursor arrivals that revealed a
     **solid inner core**; the PKIKP phase; later confirmation via free oscillations,
     and the inner core's anisotropy and possible differential rotation as live
     research.
   - **The 410 km and 660 km discontinuities** as mineral **phase transitions**
     (olivine → wadsleyite → ringwoodite → bridgmanite + ferropericlase), verified in
     the laboratory, and why a phase change makes a seismic reflector without any
     change in bulk composition. Mention the discovery of hydrous ringwoodite in a
     natural diamond as physical confirmation of water in the transition zone.
   - **Seismic tomography** (1970s–80s to today): how thousands of crisscrossing ray
     paths invert into a three-dimensional velocity model, and what tomography
     actually shows — subducted slabs traceable into the lower mantle, the two
     **Large Low-Shear-Velocity Provinces** beneath Africa and the Pacific, and
     ultralow-velocity zones at the base of the mantle.
   - **Extraterrestrial seismology as proof the method travels:** the Apollo Passive
     Seismic Experiment and lunar moonquakes; the **InSight** mission's SEIS
     instrument on Mars, its detection of hundreds of marsquakes, its measurement of
     crustal thickness, and crucially its detection of a **large liquid core** — plus
     the fact that InSight found **no evidence of plate tectonics** on Mars. This is
     the best demonstration that a landed seismometer is the decisive instrument for
     the question this chapter asks; report it with published numbers and
     uncertainties.
4. **The Earth's thermal and rheological structure.** Global heat flow (~46–47 TW)
   and its continental versus oceanic partition; the geotherm and the **adiabatic**
   gradient; the distinction between compositional layers (crust, mantle, core) and
   mechanical ones (**lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere**), and why the
   mechanical division is the one that matters for tectonics. Explain **viscosity**
   in a solid that flows on geological timescales, the Arrhenius-type temperature
   dependence
   $$\eta(T)\propto\exp\!\left(\frac{E_a}{RT}\right)$$
   and its extreme sensitivity — a few hundred kelvin changes viscosity by orders of
   magnitude — plus pressure dependence, the effect of water and melt on rheology,
   and diffusion versus dislocation creep. Give measured mantle viscosity estimates
   (upper mantle ~$10^{19-21}$, lower mantle ~$10^{21-23}$ Pa·s) and how postglacial
   rebound constrains them.
5. **Convection: the criterion, the scaling and the force balance.** Present the
   **Rayleigh number**
   $$\mathrm{Ra}=\frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T d^{3}}{\eta \kappa}$$
   defining every symbol with units, and explain each factor as a competition between
   buoyancy and dissipation. State the **critical Rayleigh number** for the onset of
   convection (order $10^3$, exact value depending on boundary conditions) and
   Earth's estimated mantle $\mathrm{Ra}$ (order $10^{6}$–$10^{8}$), and hence why
   Earth's mantle convects vigorously. Explain the **Nusselt number** and the
   $\mathrm{Nu}\propto\mathrm{Ra}^{\beta}$ scaling with $\beta\approx1/3$, and what it
   implies: **a hotter, lower-viscosity mantle sheds heat more efficiently** — the key
   to thermal-evolution arguments, including the classic "thermal catastrophe"
   problem when Earth's history is integrated backwards. Then the mechanics of plate
   motion specifically: the plate as the **cold upper thermal boundary layer of the
   convecting system** rather than something riding passively on it, and the force
   balance — **slab pull** dominant, **ridge push** secondary, basal drag, plate
   bending dissipation, and the observed correlation between a plate's velocity and
   the length of subducting slab attached to it. Include the boundary-layer scaling
   for oceanic lithosphere thickening with age ($\propto\sqrt{t}$) and the
   corresponding depth-versus-age and heat-flow-versus-age relations, the cleanest
   quantitative confirmations of the model.
6. **Plate tectonics: what the theory asserts, and the evidence that convinced
   people.** Wegener's continental drift and why it was rejected (no mechanism,
   implausible forces); Holmes's convection proposal; then the decisive post-war
   evidence chain: **ocean-floor bathymetry** and the mid-ocean ridge system; Hess
   and Dietz on **seafloor spreading**; the **Vine–Matthews–Morley** hypothesis and
   magnetic-stripe symmetry across ridges; palaeomagnetism, apparent polar wander and
   reversal chronology; deep-sea drilling confirming that ocean crust ages
   monotonically away from ridges and that **no ocean floor is older than
   ~180–200 Myr** while continental cratons reach ~4 Gyr; **Wadati–Benioff zones** of
   earthquakes dipping beneath arcs; Wilson's transform faults and hotspot tracks;
   and finally direct **GPS/GNSS geodesy** measuring plate velocities of a few
   centimetres per year in real time. Then the anatomy: divergent, convergent and
   transform boundaries; the **Wilson cycle**; ocean–ocean, ocean–continent and
   continent–continent convergence and the distinct landforms each produces (island
   arc, Andean-type cordillera, Himalayan-type collisional belt); accretionary versus
   collisional orogens; **isostasy** and crustal roots (Airy versus Pratt), which is
   *why* a mountain range implies thickened low-density crust; and the origin of
   Earth's **bimodal hypsometry** in the existence of two crust types of different
   density. Also cover the **compositional requirement**: continental crust is
   granitic and buoyant, and making granite requires hydrous partial melting of
   basalt — so continents are themselves a tectonic and hydrological statement, not
   merely a topographic one.
7. **The regime question — the transferable core of the chapter.** Present the modern
   comparative-planetology framing: mantle convection in a rocky body organises into
   distinct regimes, and which one occurs is set by the ratio of convective stress to
   lithospheric **yield strength**, usually expressed through a yield-stress or
   plastic-failure parameter in convection models. Cover:
   - **Mobile lid / plate tectonics** — the lid fails, subducts and recycles.
     Requirements identified in the literature: sufficiently weak or damaged
     lithosphere, low enough fault friction, water (hydration weakening,
     serpentinisation, pore pressure), a sufficiently cool surface, and adequate body
     size. Discuss the **initiation problem** honestly: breaking an intact lid is
     genuinely hard, which is why subduction initiation is still an open research
     question and why the onset of plate tectonics on Earth is disputed across a range
     from the Hadean to the Neoproterozoic. Summarise the competing evidence lines
     (zircon geochemistry, first appearance of eclogite and blueschist,
     palaeomagnetic tests of plate motion, onset of the modern Wilson cycle) and
     report the disagreement as disagreement.
   - **Stagnant lid** — the lithosphere never breaks; convection continues beneath
     it; heat escapes by conduction through a thick immobile shell plus volcanism.
     Use **Venus** as the primary case: nearly Earth's size and heat budget, a mean
     surface age of only ~200–1,000 Myr, ~1,000 coronae, no ridge system, no bimodal
     hypsometry, no evidence of large-scale subduction — and the leading explanations
     (a dry lithosphere with no water to weaken it, a hot surface reducing the
     thermal gradient and thus failure stress, possible episodic global resurfacing,
     and newer "squishy lid" / lithospheric-dripping and localised-deformation
     proposals plus recent claimed evidence of ongoing tectonic and volcanic
     activity). Add **Mars** (one-plate planet; the Tharsis bulge and Olympus Mons as
     products of a long-lived stationary hotspot beneath a stagnant lid; Valles
     Marineris as extensional rifting without spreading; InSight's confirmation) and
     **Mercury** and the **Moon** (global contractional features from cooling, lobate
     scarps, wrinkle ridges).
   - **Sluggish lid** and **episodic overturn** as intermediate regimes in the
     modelling literature, and what observable each predicts.
   - **Heat-pipe / advective resurfacing** — the crucial regime for this chapter.
     Explain the physics: at very high heat flux, melt carries heat through the
     lithosphere far more efficiently than conduction; magma ascends through narrow
     conduits, erupts, and the resulting surface layers are buried and carried
     **downward** by continuous burial, so the lid thickens and stays cold and strong
     while the entire heat budget vents volcanically. Cite the literature that
     developed this for **Io** and for the early Earth and early terrestrial planets.
     Give Io's numbers: tidal heat output of order $10^{14}$ W (~100 TW, roughly
     2 W/m², over twenty times Earth's), 400-plus active volcanoes, resurfacing rates
     of order centimetres per year, essentially **no impact craters**, and mountains
     up to ~18 km high that are **fault-bounded blocks produced by compression from
     burial subsidence, not by plate collision**, with no ridges, no arcs and no
     subduction zones. This is the chapter's pivot and must be documented precisely
     and with sources: **Io proves a tidally heated world can build tall relief
     without plate tectonics, by an entirely different mechanism.** Explain that
     mechanism (compressive stress from continuous burial and the resulting thrust
     faulting) carefully enough that the chapter can offer it as a serious
     alternative hypothesis for Pandora's ranges.
   - The **regime map**: what is known about how body size, heat flux, surface
     temperature, water content and mantle rheology jointly select a regime; the
     literature on super-Earth and exoplanet tectonics — including the fact that it
     disagrees with itself about whether larger planets are more or less likely to
     have plate tectonics, so report both sides; and what a **tidally heated,
     water-rich, roughly Earth-sized** body would be predicted to do, since that is
     Pandora's parameter combination and it has no Solar System representative.
8. **The diagnostics — how you would *test* which regime a world is in.** Assemble an
   explicit observational checklist, because the chapter's method section depends on
   it: crater density and surface-age distribution; hypsometry (bimodal versus
   unimodal); a linear ridge system with symmetric magnetic striping; age progression
   of ocean floor; arcs and Wadati–Benioff seismicity; heat-flow-versus-age
   relations; the presence of granitic/felsic continental crust and of metamorphic
   rocks that require subduction geotherms (**eclogite, blueschist**); long-lived
   hotspot tracks (which indicate a moving lid over a stationary source, and so are
   diagnostic even without subduction); geodetic surface velocities; tomographic
   detection of slabs; and the existence of an intrinsic dynamo (plate tectonics cools
   a core efficiently and favours a dynamo, though it is neither necessary nor
   sufficient). For each diagnostic, state what a *positive* and a *negative* result
   would license.
9. **Ore genesis — why the mining question is really a tectonics question.** Explain
   how Earth concentrates elements from crustal abundances of parts per million to
   minable grade, and how strongly each mechanism depends on crustal mobility and
   fluid flow: **magmatic segregation** (layered intrusions, chromite and PGE reefs,
   magmatic Ni–Cu sulfides); **hydrothermal systems** (porphyry copper at
   subduction-related arcs, epithermal gold, volcanogenic massive sulfides at
   spreading centres and black smokers, orogenic gold in collisional belts,
   Mississippi Valley-type deposits); **metamorphic and orogenic** remobilisation;
   **sedimentary and surficial** processes (placers, banded iron formations,
   laterites, evaporites); and **pegmatites** and highly evolved granites for the rare
   elements. Give the enrichment factors involved. The key argument for the chapter:
   on Earth the richest and most systematically located ore provinces are **products
   of plate boundaries and of long-lived water-driven crustal recycling**, which is
   why terrestrial ore deposits form in predictable belts. State what is known and
   unknown about ore formation on one-plate bodies (lunar and Martian resource
   studies; why the Moon and Mars lack hydrothermal ore provinces comparable to
   Earth's) and what a stagnant-lid or heat-pipe world could still plausibly produce
   (magmatic segregation, sublimate deposits, impact-related concentration, and Io's
   sulfur volcanism as an exotic case).
10. **Honest limits and open problems.** Be explicit about what solid-Earth science
    still argues over, so the chapter can be calibrated rather than confident: when
    plate tectonics began on Earth and whether it was gradual or episodic; whether
    Venus is currently mobile in any limited sense; whether water is strictly
    necessary for plate tectonics or merely strongly favourable; the true nature of
    the LLSVPs; how subduction initiates; the mantle's water budget; how far plate
    tectonics is actually required for a long-lived biosphere (the habitability
    argument via the carbonate–silicate thermostat and nutrient recycling, and the
    counter-argument that stagnant-lid worlds might remain habitable); and the
    disagreement in the exoplanet literature about super-Earth tectonics.
11. **A bounded Pandora tectonic model.** End with the analytical synthesis the
    chapter will lean on. First a parameter table separating (a) values established by
    official canon; (b) values estimable from canon plus physics — for example mean
    density and hence rock/metal fraction from stated gravity and radius, an estimated
    mantle Rayleigh number range given the hundreds-of-terawatts tidal budget
    established in the volcanism chapter, an implied surface heat flux in W/m², and a
    comparison of that flux to Earth's ~0.087 W/m² and Io's ~2 W/m²; (c) values
    entirely unknown and unsafe to assume; and (d) qualitative conclusions robust
    across the plausible range. Then lay out **explicitly tiered competing
    hypotheses** rather than one falsely precise answer, and for each state what it
    predicts, what canon observation supports or strains it, and what measurement
    would discriminate it:
    - **(H1) Earth-like mobile lid.** Pandora has plates; its ranges are collisional
      or arc orogens; its ore provinces are hydrothermal and arc-related; its oceans
      sit on a distinct, denser crust. Strongest support: it is wet, it has ranges, it
      has minable ore. Strain: the tidal heat budget may leave the lithosphere too
      thin and weak to behave as a coherent plate, and no canonical arc, ridge or
      fault has ever been shown.
    - **(H2) Heat-pipe / Io-like advective regime.** Pandora vents its tidal heat
      volcanically, resurfaces rapidly, and its mountains are burial-compression fault
      blocks as Io's are. Support: the tidal budget, the volcanic province, the absence
      of any canonical tectonic landform. Strain: rapid global resurfacing is hard to
      reconcile with an ancient, continuous, deeply interconnected biosphere, with
      thick soils and with old forests, and Io-style volcanism would repeatedly

    - **(H3) Stagnant or sluggish lid with vigorous interior convection.** Ranges are
      volcanic constructs and fault-block uplifts; ore is magmatic and sublimate;
      ocean basins are impact or subsidence features. Support: this is the modal
      outcome for rocky bodies in the Solar System. Strain: it makes ore-grade
      unobtanium harder to explain and offers no source of granitic continental crust.
    - **(H4) Hybrid or episodic regime** — regional mobility, plume-driven
      lithospheric dripping, or episodic overturn separated by long quiescent
      intervals, which could reconcile a stable biosphere with a high heat budget.
      State what modelling literature exists for such regimes and how weakly
      constrained it is.
    Finish by naming the **single most decisive measurement** an expedition could make

    lid that is *part of* the convecting system rather than a raft upon it — but no
