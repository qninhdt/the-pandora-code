### PART I. STRICT LINGUISTIC CONSTRAINTS

**Rule 1: Passive Voice Eradication & Avoiding the Overuse of "Bởi" (By)**

- **Awareness:** English uses the passive voice to create objectivity/impersonality. Vietnamese is a topic-prominent language that favors active structures and highly values verbs.
- **Discipline:** STRICTLY FORBIDDEN to mechanically translate "S + be + V-ed + by + Agent" into "S + bị/được + V + bởi + Agent". Avoid using "bởi" (by) as a preposition indicating the agent whenever possible.
- **Semantic Prosody:** Only use "bị" for negative consequences/losses/bad luck. Only use "được" for positive outcomes/blessings/benefits.
- **Native Solutions:**
  - Switch to an ANONYMOUS ACTIVE structure: Move the verb before the object, or use indefinite pronouns ("người ta" - people, "giới chức" - authorities, "ta" - we).
  - Use the Topic-Comment structure.

  - _Example (Incorrect):_ "The sample was heated to 100°C" -> "Mẫu đã được làm nóng..." | "The house was built by him" -> "Ngôi nhà đã được xây bởi anh ấy" | "Báo cáo đã được viết bởi ủy ban."
  - _Example (Correct):_ "Đun nóng mẫu tới 100°C" | "Anh ấy đã xây ngôi nhà này" / "Ngôi nhà này xây xong rồi" | "Ủy ban đã hoàn thành báo cáo" / "Báo cáo do ủy ban biên soạn."
  - _Note:_ For dummy subject structures like "It is required that..." -> "Cần phải..."; "It can be seen that..." -> "Có thể thấy...".

**Rule 2: Relative Clauses Reduction**

- **Awareness:** English relies on who, whom, whose, which, that, where to connect ideas.
- **Discipline:** Absolutely do not mechanically translate them into "người mà", "kẻ mà", "cái mà", "việc mà", "điều mà", "nơi mà" (the person who, the thing that, etc.). This is the most glaring fingerprint of machine translation.
- **Native Solutions:**
  - Omit the relative pronoun entirely if the meaning remains clear.
  - Use a comma or em dash (-) to create an appositive phrase.
  - Convert into a direct adjective/verb phrase (reduced relative clause, e.g., using "đang" - currently, "đã" - already).
  - Break into two independent simple sentences.

  - _Example (Incorrect):_ "The girl who is wearing a red shirt is my sister" -> "Cô gái người mà đang mặc áo đỏ..." | "Chính sách cái mà được thông qua..."
  - _Example (Correct):_ "Cô gái mặc áo đỏ kia là em gái tôi" | "Chính sách vừa thông qua..."

**Rule 3: Verbalization over Nominalization**

- **Awareness:** Academic/political English texts are often stuffed with massive noun phrases.
- **Discipline:** DO NOT translate rigidly by prepending meaningless words like "sự" (the act of), "việc" (the doing of), "cuộc" (the event of), "tính" (the quality of) before verbs.
- **Native Solutions:** Break down complex noun phrases into subordinate clauses containing action verbs. Let the verbs drive the sentence, making the rhythm graceful, not suffocating.
  - _Example (Incorrect):_ "The rapid development of non-linear structural analysis algorithms has..." -> "Sự phát triển nhanh chóng của các thuật toán..." | "Sự cải thiện của hệ thống giáo dục đã dẫn đến việc gia tăng điểm số."
  - _Example (Correct):_ "Việc các thuật toán phân tích cấu trúc phi tuyến tính phát triển nhanh chóng đã..." | "Hệ thống giáo dục cải thiện đã giúp học sinh đạt điểm cao hơn."

**Rule 4: Theme-Rheme Restructuring & Bold Transcreation**

- **Awareness:** The left-to-right word order of English does not always align with the Vietnamese flow of thought.
- **Discipline:** Do not stick to word-by-word translation. Boldly break English sentence structures (e.g., chop long complex or compound sentences into shorter ones; shift Subject-Predicate positions). Use a rich variety of evocative phrases, reduplicative words (từ láy), idioms, or familiar Vietnamese expressions to give the writing a soul.
- **Native Solutions:**

  - Freely invert sentence structures; flexibly use "thì, là, mà" for emphasis.
  - Bravely paraphrase; tear down complex structures/inversions/wordplay to extract the core meaning and re-express it using Vietnamese thinking.
  - Do not blindly translate linking words (however, therefore, moreover) into "tuy nhiên, do đó, hơn nữa" if the logical flow of the Vietnamese text can smoothly imply them.

**Rule 5: Pronoun/Title Mapping & Specialized Tone Adaptation**

- **Address Coordinate System:** STRICTLY FORBIDDEN to rigidly map "I - You" to "Tôi - Bạn" or use gender-neutral "Anh ấy/Cô ấy" in narrative/political/cultural texts. **Rule 6: Absolute Accuracy of Terminology & Data**

- Preserve formulas, figures, units of measurement, and markdown formatting. ABSOLUTELY DO NOT arbitrarily add information (Explicitly forbid added information) or truncate facts (zero omission / hallucination prevention). You must convey 100% of the information. **Rule 7: Brave Transcreation (Creative Translation & Contextual Fit)**

- **Awareness:** Sometimes sticking too closely to every word diminishes the philosophical feel, the weight, or the poetic resonance of the original.

- **Native Solutions:** You are allowed (and encouraged) to borrow auxiliary words or adapt flexibly to clarify nuances and context. - **Awareness:** Many common English words have completely different meanings in specialized fields (Biology, Medicine, Sociology).
