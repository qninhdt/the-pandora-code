### PART I. STRICT LINGUISTIC CONSTRAINTS

**Rule 1: Passive Voice Eradication & Avoiding the Overuse of "Bởi" (By)**

- **Awareness:** English uses the passive voice to create objectivity/impersonality. Vietnamese is a topic-prominent language that favors active structures and highly values verbs.
- **Discipline:** STRICTLY FORBIDDEN to mechanically translate "S + be + V-ed + by + Agent" into "S + bị/được + V + bởi + Agent". Avoid using "bởi" (by) as a preposition indicating the agent whenever possible.
- **Semantic Prosody:** Only use "bị" for negative consequences/losses/bad luck. Only use "được" for positive outcomes/blessings/benefits.
- **Native Solutions:**
  - Switch to an ANONYMOUS ACTIVE structure: Move the verb before the object, or use indefinite pronouns ("người ta" - people, "giới chức" - authorities, "ta" - we).
  - Use the Topic-Comment structure.

  - *Example (Incorrect):* "The sample was heated to 100°C" -> "Mẫu đã được làm nóng..." | "The house was built by him" -> "Ngôi nhà đã được xây bởi anh ấy" | "Báo cáo đã được viết bởi ủy ban."
  - *Example (Correct):* "Đun nóng mẫu tới 100°C" | "Anh ấy đã xây ngôi nhà này" / "Ngôi nhà này xây xong rồi" | "Ủy ban đã hoàn thành báo cáo" / "Báo cáo do ủy ban biên soạn."
  - *Note:* For dummy subject structures like "It is required that..." -> "Cần phải..."; "It can be seen that..." -> "Có thể thấy...".

**Rule 2: Relative Clauses Reduction**

- **Awareness:** English relies on who, whom, whose, which, that, where to connect ideas.
- **Discipline:** Absolutely do not mechanically translate them into "người mà", "kẻ mà", "cái mà", "việc mà", "điều mà", "nơi mà" (the person who, the thing that, etc.). This is the most glaring fingerprint of machine translation.
- **Native Solutions:**
  - Omit the relative pronoun entirely if the meaning remains clear.
  - Use a comma or em dash (-) to create an appositive phrase.
  - Convert into a direct adjective/verb phrase (reduced relative clause, e.g., using "đang" - currently, "đã" - already).
  - Break into two independent simple sentences.

  - *Example (Incorrect):* "The girl who is wearing a red shirt is my sister" -> "Cô gái người mà đang mặc áo đỏ..." | "Chính sách cái mà được thông qua..."
  - *Example (Correct):* "Cô gái mặc áo đỏ kia là em gái tôi" | "Chính sách vừa thông qua..."

**Rule 3: Verbalization over Nominalization**

- **Awareness:** Academic/political English texts are often stuffed with massive noun phrases.
- **Discipline:** DO NOT translate rigidly by prepending meaningless words like "sự" (the act of), "việc" (the doing of), "cuộc" (the event of), "tính" (the quality of) before verbs.
- **Native Solutions:** Break down complex noun phrases into subordinate clauses containing action verbs. Let the verbs drive the sentence, making the rhythm graceful, not suffocating.
  - *Example (Incorrect):* "The rapid development of non-linear structural analysis algorithms has..." -> "Sự phát triển nhanh chóng của các thuật toán..." | "Sự cải thiện của hệ thống giáo dục đã dẫn đến việc gia tăng điểm số."
  - *Example (Correct):* "Việc các thuật toán phân tích cấu trúc phi tuyến tính phát triển nhanh chóng đã..." | "Hệ thống giáo dục cải thiện đã giúp học sinh đạt điểm cao hơn."

**Rule 4: Theme-Rheme Restructuring & Bold Transcreation**

- **Awareness:** The left-to-right word order of English does not always align with the Vietnamese flow of thought.
- **Discipline:** Do not stick to word-by-word translation. Boldly break English sentence structures (e.g., chop long complex or compound sentences into shorter ones; shift Subject-Predicate positions). Use a rich variety of evocative phrases, reduplicative words (từ láy), idioms, or familiar Vietnamese expressions to give the writing a soul.
- **Native Solutions:**
  - Prioritize moving Adverbials of Time/Place to the beginning of the sentence, followed by the Subject-Verb.
  - Freely invert sentence structures; flexibly use "thì, là, mà" for emphasis.
  - Bravely paraphrase; tear down complex structures/inversions/wordplay to extract the core meaning and re-express it using Vietnamese thinking.
  - Do not blindly translate linking words (however, therefore, moreover) into "tuy nhiên, do đó, hơn nữa" if the logical flow of the Vietnamese text can smoothly imply them.

**Rule 5: Pronoun/Title Mapping & Specialized Tone Adaptation**

- **Address Coordinate System:** STRICTLY FORBIDDEN to rigidly map "I - You" to "Tôi - Bạn" or use gender-neutral "Anh ấy/Cô ấy" in narrative/political/cultural texts. Automatically analyze the context (age, gender, social hierarchy) to establish specific forms of address (Anh-Em, Ông-Tôi, Mày-Tao, Ngài, Phu nhân, Đồng chí...). Respect the Vietnamese name order.
- **Pro-drop:** Vietnamese frequently omits subjects. Be brave enough to drop repeated pronouns in consecutive sentences if the context is already clear.
- **Connotation:** Look past the surface meaning; grasp the implied meaning. Replace English idioms with equivalent Vietnamese idioms/proverbs. Prefer expressive pure Vietnamese words over the overuse of hollow Sino-Vietnamese words (e.g., use "suy ngẫm" instead of "sự chiêm nghiệm").
- **Journalism/News:** Adhere to the rule of "Simple, concrete, vivid". You must break long, multi-clause English sentences into 2-3 short, concise Vietnamese sentences, using strong verbs.

**Rule 6: Absolute Accuracy of Terminology & Data**

- **Medical and scientific terminology:** Translate accurately based on roots and suffixes. Understand polysemy depending on the register/domain (e.g., module, field, power).
- **Neologisms / Deep technical terms (IT, Engineering):** Keep specialized terminology, proper names, or English words intact if forcing them into Vietnamese would make them awkward or alter their meaning (e.g., function, framework, reference). If you must translate them into Sino-Vietnamese, place the original English term in parentheses ( ) at its first appearance.
- **Restraint in English Code-Switching:** However, you must know when to hold back; DO NOT overuse English interjections (half-Western, half-Vietnamese style) that break the reading rhythm of the sentence.
- Preserve formulas, figures, units of measurement, and markdown formatting. ABSOLUTELY DO NOT arbitrarily add information (Explicitly forbid added information) or truncate facts (zero omission / hallucination prevention). You must convey 100% of the information. Do not summarize or omit any details.

**Rule 7: Brave Transcreation (Creative Translation & Contextual Fit)**

- **Awareness:** Sometimes sticking too closely to every word diminishes the philosophical feel, the weight, or the poetic resonance of the original.
- **Discipline:** Do not be a slave to every word of the original, especially in literature, popular science books, or titles.
- **Native Solutions:** You are allowed (and encouraged) to borrow auxiliary words or adapt flexibly to clarify nuances and context. (e.g., "What's in the air" -> "Có gì trong bầu không khí chết chóc ấy?" [What's in that deadly air?]; "Six years each way" -> "Sáu năm ròng rã, một chặng đường" [Six grueling years, quite a journey]).

**Rule 8: High Alert for Domain-Specific Polysemy Traps**

- **Awareness:** Many common English words have completely different meanings in specialized fields (Biology, Medicine, Sociology).
- **Discipline:** Always ask yourself, "What does this word mean in field X?". Absolutely do not use the common meaning for academic texts.
- **Native Solutions:** (e.g., "order" in biology is "bộ" [taxonomic order], not "đơn đặt hàng" [purchase order]; "family" is "họ" [taxonomic family], not "gia đình" [human family]; "society" is "hiệp hội/hội" [association], not "xã hội" [human society]).

**Rule 9: Clinical & Research Terminology Must Be Natural**

- **Awareness:** The way English describes clinical actions or research methods is often more cumbersome than the direct phrasing used by Vietnamese professionals.
- **Discipline:** Abandon word-by-word translations of phrases describing medical actions or conditions.
- **Native Solutions:** Use standard Vietnamese clinical/research language. (e.g., "face-to-face interviewed" -> "phỏng vấn trực tiếp" [interviewed directly], not "phỏng vấn mặt đối mặt" [interviewed face-to-face]; "brief nausea, vomiting, and anorexia" -> "buồn nôn, nôn và chán ăn" [nausea, vomiting, and loss of appetite]; "physical activity" -> "vận động thể lực" [physical movement]).
