# Canon Policy

What the author may assert, and on whose authority.

## The four tiers

| Tier | What it is | Source of truth |
|---|---|---|
| **canon** | Established in the films / official Avatar material | The research note's canon section (which cites official sources) |
| **inference** | Not stated, but follows from canon + sound reasoning | Author reasoning, flagged as inference |
| **speculation** | Plausible but unprovable extension | Flagged explicitly as speculation |
| **real_science** | Verifiable Earth science | The research note's science section (cites literature) |

## Rules

1. **Canon comes only from the research note.** If a Pandora fact is not in
   `research/{slug}.md` and is not real Earth science, the author does not assert
   it as fact. It can appear as clearly-marked inference or speculation, or not
   at all.
2. **Never upgrade a tier.** Community/wiki material in the note is not canon;
   treat it as inference at best, and say so. A speculation never becomes an
   inference because it's convenient.
3. **Cite the source, not the synthesizer.** Deep Research summarizes; cite the
   underlying source it points to, not the summary paragraph.
4. **Mark tiers visibly to the reader.** Use the classification components /
   badges so the reader always knows which tier a claim sits in. This honesty is
   a feature of the book.
5. **When canon is silent, say so.** "The films never show how the queue bonds at
   the cellular level" is a legitimate, interesting sentence - better than
   inventing a mechanism.

## Interaction with classification percentages

The chapter's `classification` percentages (canon/inference/speculation/
real_science, summing to 100) should honestly reflect the mix of claims in the
finished chapter. See `classification-rubric.md` for how to estimate them.
